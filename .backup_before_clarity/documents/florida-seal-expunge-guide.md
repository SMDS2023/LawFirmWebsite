## Understanding Sealing vs. Expungement

### What is Sealing?

**Sealing** is defined under Florida Statute § 943.045(19) as "the preservation of a record under such circumstances that it is secure and inaccessible to any person not having a legal right of access to the record or the information contained and preserved therein."

**Key Characteristics of Sealing:**
- The criminal record is **hidden from public view** but not destroyed
- The record remains confidential and inaccessible to most employers, landlords, and the general public
- Certain government agencies retain access under specific circumstances
- Sealed records must be disclosed when purchasing firearms or applying for concealed carry permits
- Available immediately upon case resolution or completion of court-ordered supervision

### What is Expungement?

**Expungement** is defined under Florida Statute § 943.0585 as "the court-ordered physical destruction or obliteration of a record or portion of a record by any criminal justice agency having custody thereof."

**Key Characteristics of Expungement:**
- Criminal records are **physically destroyed or obliterated** by all agencies
- Provides the highest level of privacy protection
- Only a confidential copy is retained by the Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE)
- Expunged records do NOT need to be disclosed when purchasing firearms or applying for concealed carry permits
- Generally available only after charges are dismissed, dropped, or result in acquittal (or after a record has been sealed for 10 years)

### Critical Differences

| **Aspect** | **Sealing** | **Expungement** |
|------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Record Status** | Hidden but preserved | Physically destroyed |
| **Public Access** | No public access | No public access |
| **Government Access** | Certain agencies retain access | Only FDLE retains confidential copy |
| **Firearm Disclosure** | Must be disclosed | Does NOT need to be disclosed |
| **Eligibility** | Withheld adjudication cases | Dismissed/dropped charges or sealed for 10+ years |
| **Privacy Level** | High | Highest |

### The Right to Deny Your Criminal History

Both sealing and expungement allow you to **lawfully deny or fail to acknowledge** the arrest or criminal record in most circumstances, including:
- Employment applications (with certain exceptions)
- Housing applications
- Educational applications
- Most professional licensing applications

**Important Exceptions:** You MUST disclose sealed or expunged records when:
- Applying for employment with criminal justice agencies
- Seeking certain professional licenses (teaching, law enforcement)
- Applying to own or operate a care facility
- Running for public office
- Involved in subsequent criminal proceedings
- Seeking to seal or expunge another record

## Eligibility Requirements

### General Eligibility Criteria

To qualify for either sealing or expungement in Florida, you must meet ALL of the following requirements:

#### 1. **One-Time Opportunity**
Florida law allows you to seal or expunge **only ONE criminal record in your lifetime**. If you have previously sealed or expunged a record in Florida, you are automatically disqualified from doing so again. (Prior actions in other states do not affect Florida eligibility.)

#### 2. **No Prior Convictions**
You must never have been **adjudicated guilty** in Florida of any criminal offense. Out-of-state convictions generally do not disqualify you.

#### 3. **No Pending Charges**
You cannot have any pending criminal charges or be under any form of court supervision (probation, community control) at the time of application.

#### 4. **Eligible Offense**
Your offense must not be on Florida's list of disqualifying offenses (see below).

### Specific Eligibility: Sealing

You may be eligible to have your record **sealed** if:
- The court **withheld adjudication** after you entered a plea of guilty or no contest
- You were never adjudicated guilty (formally convicted)
- You have completed all terms of probation or court-ordered supervision
- The offense is not on the list of ineligible offenses under § 943.0584
- Sealing is available **immediately** upon completion of court-ordered supervision

### Specific Eligibility: Expungement

You may be eligible to have your record **expunged** if:
- Your charges were **dismissed, dropped, or not prosecuted** (nolle prosequi), OR
- You were **found not guilty** at trial, OR
- A previously sealed record has been sealed for **at least 10 years**

**Exception:** Even if adjudication was withheld or you were found not guilty, you cannot expunge until the record has been sealed for 10 years, UNLESS no plea was entered or charges were dismissed before trial.

### Disqualifying Offenses (Per § 943.0584, F.S.)

The following offenses are **NEVER eligible** for sealing or expungement, even if adjudication was withheld:

**Violent Crimes:**
- Murder, Manslaughter, Homicide
- Aggravated Assault or Aggravated Battery
- Felony Battery
- Kidnapping or False Imprisonment
- Carjacking or Robbery (all forms)
- Home-Invasion Robbery
- Assault or Battery (domestic violence)
- Domestic Battery by Strangulation

**Sex Offenses:**
- Sexual Battery (all Chapter 794 offenses)
- Lewd or Lascivious Offenses (involving persons under 16)
- Sexual Performance by a Child
- Computer Pornography
- Any offense requiring sex offender registration

**Crimes Against Children:**
- Child Abuse or Aggravated Child Abuse
- Luring or Enticing a Child
- Selling or Buying of Minors

**Serious Property Crimes:**
- Arson
- Burglary of a Dwelling

**Drug Crimes:**
- Drug Trafficking
- Manufacturing Controlled Substances

**Other Serious Offenses:**
- Human Trafficking
- Abuse of an Elderly or Disabled Person
- Offenses by Public Officers/Employees (Chapter 839)
- Aircraft Piracy
- Any predicate offense for sexual predator/offender registration

## The Step-by-Step Process

Successfully sealing or expunging your criminal record involves multiple stages, each requiring careful attention to detail. Here's what to expect:

### Phase 1: Eligibility Assessment (1-7 Days)

**What Happens:**
- Review your complete criminal history
- Identify the specific case(s) you wish to seal or expunge
- Verify eligibility based on statutory requirements
- Determine whether sealing or expungement is appropriate
- Gather preliminary case information

**With an Attorney:** Many experienced expungement firms offer **free eligibility reviews** and can assess your case within minutes to days, ensuring you don't waste time and money pursuing relief you're not eligible for.

### Phase 2: Certificate of Eligibility Application (2-4 Months)

**What Happens:**
The Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE) must issue a **Certificate of Eligibility** before you can petition the court. This is a mandatory first step.

**Required Documents and Steps:**

#### For ALL Applications (Sealing and Expungement):

1. **Complete FDLE Application Form (FDLE 40-021)**
   - Download from FDLE website or request via email at SEinfo@fdle.state.fl.us
   - Complete Section A in its entirety
   - Sign the application **under oath** in the presence of a notary public

2. **Submit Fingerprints**
   - Must be taken by authorized law enforcement personnel
   - Use standard fingerprint card
   - Include applicant's name, race, sex, date of birth, social security number, and signature

3. **Provide Certified Disposition(s)**
   - Obtain from the Clerk of Court in the county where charges originated
   - Must show final outcome of case (dismissal, nolle prosequi, not guilty, withheld adjudication, etc.)
   - If you completed probation, include certified copy of probation termination

4. **Pay FDLE Processing Fee**
   - **$75** (non-refundable)
   - Must be money order, cashier's check, or personal check payable to FDLE
   - NO cash, gift cards, or temporary checks accepted

#### For EXPUNGEMENT Applications ONLY:

5. **State Attorney Review and Approval (Section B)**
   - **Before submitting to FDLE**, you must deliver the application to the State Attorney's Office in the county where you were charged
   - The State Attorney completes Section B, certifying whether the case is eligible for expungement
   - This review can take **2-6 weeks** depending on the office's workload
   - The State Attorney will return the completed application to you by mail

**Submission to FDLE:**
Mail all required documents to:
```
Florida Department of Law Enforcement
Attn: Expunge/Seal Section
P.O. Box 1489
Tallahassee, FL 32302-1489
```

**Processing Time:** FDLE states that processing typically takes **12 weeks (90 business days)** from the date a complete application is received. However, actual processing times can range from **2-6 months** depending on:
- Current backlog at FDLE
- Completeness and accuracy of your application
- Complexity of your criminal history
- Whether additional research is required

**Important Notes:**
- FDLE does **NOT expedite** applications
- Applications are processed in the order received
- If your application is incomplete, FDLE will return it, causing significant delays
- The Certificate of Eligibility is valid for **12 months** from the date of issuance

### Phase 3: Filing the Court Petition (1-4 Weeks After Receiving Certificate)

**What Happens:**
Once you receive your Certificate of Eligibility from FDLE, you must file a formal petition with the court where the charges originated.

**Required Documents:**

1. **Petition to Expunge or Seal**
   - Must be completed accurately with all case information
   - Must be signed by the petitioner

2. **Sworn Affidavit**
   - Must be notarized
   - Attests to your eligibility under Florida law
   - Certifies you have no other pending petitions

3. **Certificate of Eligibility from FDLE**
   - Original document

4. **Proposed Order**
   - For the judge to sign if petition is granted

**Filing Process:**
- File original documents with the Clerk of Court in the county where the arrest occurred
- Pay court filing fee (typically **$42**, varies by county)
- Submit copies to:
  - State Attorney's Office (county where case originated)
  - Arresting agency
  - FDLE (if required by local rules)

**Professional Assistance:** An experienced attorney will:
- Prepare all documents with precise legal language
- Ensure all technical requirements are met
- Coordinate with the Clerk of Court for proper filing
- Send certified copies to all required agencies
- Avoid common errors that lead to rejection or delay

### Phase 4: State Attorney Review (2-6 Weeks)

**What Happens:**
After you file the petition with the court and serve copies on the State Attorney's Office, the prosecutor reviews your case and files a response.

**Possible Outcomes:**
1. **No Objection:** The State Attorney agrees with the petition (most common if you received a Certificate of Eligibility)
2. **Objection:** The State Attorney opposes the petition (rare, but possible)

**If No Objection:**
- The Clerk forwards all documents to the judge
- The judge typically signs the order **without a hearing** within 2-6 weeks
- You receive a certified copy of the signed order

**If Objection Filed:**
- The court schedules a **hearing**
- You (or your attorney) must appear before the judge
- You present evidence and legal arguments supporting your petition
- The judge makes a decision after the hearing

### Phase 5: Court Order and Record Clearance (2-8 Weeks)

**What Happens:**
Once the judge signs the order to seal or expunge:

1. **Order Distribution (Immediate)**
   - Clerk of Court distributes certified copies to:
     - State Attorney's Office
     - Arresting agency
     - FDLE
     - FBI (via FDLE)
     - Any other agency with the record

2. **Agency Compliance (Up to 60 Days)**
   - All agencies have **up to 60 days** to comply with the court order
   - Most agencies comply within **2-4 weeks**
   - **Sealing:** Agencies mark records as sealed and restrict access
   - **Expungement:** Agencies physically destroy or obliterate records

3. **Verification**
   - You can request verification from FDLE that the order has been processed
   - Your Florida criminal history record should reflect the sealing/expungement

## Special Circumstances

### Human Trafficking Victim Expungement

**Special Provision:** Florida Statute § 943.0583 allows victims of human trafficking to petition for expungement of criminal records resulting from offenses committed as part of the trafficking scheme.

**Key Features:**
- **Does NOT require** a Certificate of Eligibility from FDLE
- **Entirely judicial proceeding** (file directly with the court)
- Available for offenses committed "as part of a human trafficking scheme" or "at the direction of an operator"
- Includes prostitution and related offenses (Chapters 796 and 847)
- **Determination by preponderance of the evidence**
- Conviction is deemed **vacated due to substantive defect** in underlying proceedings

**Exceptions:** Does NOT apply to offenses listed in § 775.084(1)(b)1 (serious violent crimes)

**How to Apply:**
1. File petition directly with circuit court (no FDLE application required)
2. Provide evidence of human trafficking victim status
3. Show offense was committed as part of trafficking scheme
4. Court makes determination and issues order

**Legal Assistance Recommended:** Due to the complexity of proving victim status and the connection to the trafficking scheme, working with an experienced attorney significantly improves the likelihood of success.

### Administrative Expungement

**What It Is:** Florida Statute § 943.0581 allows for administrative expungement of arrests made "contrary to law or by mistake."

**When Available:**
- Arrest was made in error (wrong person)
- Arrest was made without legal basis
- Arrest violated constitutional rights
- Identity theft (someone else used your information)

**Process:**
- Apply directly to FDLE (not a court proceeding)
- No Certificate of Eligibility required
- FDLE reviews and makes determination
- If approved, record is expunged administratively

### Juvenile Expungement

**Early Juvenile Expunction:** Florida offers more lenient expungement provisions for juvenile records under certain circumstances.

**Key Differences:**
- May be eligible even with adjudication of delinquency (in some cases)
- Shorter waiting periods
- More flexible eligibility criteria

**Note:** Juvenile adjudications for serious felonies may still disqualify you from adult expungement.

### Expungement After 10-Year Sealing

**Special Rule:** If your record has been sealed for **at least 10 years**, you may petition to expunge it, even if:
- Adjudication was withheld
- You pled guilty or no contest

**Requirements:**
- Record has been sealed for 10+ consecutive years
- No criminal violations during the 10-year period
- Still meet all other eligibility requirements

**Benefits of Upgrading to Expungement:**
- Higher level of privacy (record destroyed)
- No firearm disclosure requirement
- Greater peace of mind

## Frequently Asked Questions

### General Questions

**Q: Can I seal or expunge multiple arrests or cases?**
A: Florida law generally allows for only one criminal record to be sealed or expunged in a lifetime. However, if multiple arrests or charges stem from a "single arrest or incident of alleged criminal activity," they may be sealed or expunged together in one proceeding.

**Q: Will sealing or expunging my record remove it from Google and background check websites?**
A: The court order directs criminal justice agencies to seal or expunge the official record. However, **it does not automatically remove information from private websites, mugshot sites, or news articles**. Many expungement attorneys offer additional services to help remove mugshots and online records.

**Q: How long is the Certificate of Eligibility valid?**
A: The Certificate of Eligibility is valid for **12 months** from the date of issuance. If you do not file your court petition within that time, you must apply for a new certificate (and pay the $75 fee again).

**Q: Can I be denied even if I receive a Certificate of Eligibility?**
A: Yes. The Certificate of Eligibility only means you meet the statutory requirements for sealing or expungement. The judge still has discretion to deny the petition based on the facts of your case or objections from the State Attorney.

**Q: What happens if the State Attorney objects to my petition?**
A: If the State Attorney objects, the court will schedule a hearing. You (or your attorney) must present evidence and legal arguments supporting your petition. The judge will make a final decision after considering both sides.

**Q: Can I expedite the FDLE process?**
A: No. FDLE explicitly states that they do **not expedite** applications. All applications are processed in the order received.

### Employment and Background Checks

**Q: Will employers see my sealed or expunged record?**
A: Most employers will **not** see sealed or expunged records on background checks. However, there are exceptions for certain employers (criminal justice agencies, schools, nursing homes, etc.) who may have access to sealed records.

**Q: Can I legally say "no" when asked if I've ever been arrested?**
A: Yes, in most situations. Florida law allows you to lawfully deny or fail to acknowledge the arrest covered by the sealed or expunged record. **Important exceptions:** You must disclose when applying for certain jobs (law enforcement, teaching) or licenses.

**Q: What should I say if an interviewer asks about a sealed or expunged record?**
A: You can lawfully state that you have never been arrested or convicted of a crime (unless required to disclose under one of the exceptions). Consult with your attorney for guidance on specific situations.

### Eligibility and Process

**Q: I was placed on probation and adjudication was withheld. Am I eligible?**
A: If adjudication was withheld and you successfully completed probation, you are likely eligible for **sealing** (not expungement, unless the record is sealed for 10 years first). The offense must not be on the list of disqualifying offenses.

**Q: My case was dismissed. Do I qualify for expungement?**
A: If your case was dismissed (nolle prosequi, dropped, or charges not filed) and you meet all other eligibility requirements, you likely qualify for **expungement**.

**Q: Can I seal or expunge a DUI?**
A: It depends on the outcome. If you were convicted of DUI, it **cannot** be sealed or expunged. However, if charges were **reduced to reckless driving** with adjudication withheld, you may be able to seal the reckless driving record. If DUI charges were **dismissed entirely**, you may be eligible for expungement.

**Q: I have a conviction in another state. Does that disqualify me in Florida?**
A: Generally, **no**. Florida considers only Florida adjudications of guilt for eligibility purposes. Out-of-state convictions typically do not disqualify you. However, consult with an attorney to review your specific situation.

**Q: Can I seal or expunge federal charges?**
A: No. Florida's sealing and expungement statutes apply only to state criminal records. Federal offenses are governed by separate federal law and procedures.

### Costs and Attorneys

**Q: How much does it cost to seal or expunge a record in Florida?**
A: The total cost ranges from **$900 to $1,500** when hiring an attorney (including all fees). Self-filing costs **$200-$350** but has a much higher rejection rate.

**Q: Do I need an attorney, or can I do it myself?**

**Q: Are payment plans available?**

### After the Order is Granted

**Q: How long does it take for agencies to comply with the court order?**
A: Agencies have up to **60 days** to comply with a court order to seal or expunge records. Most agencies comply within **2-4 weeks**.

**Q: Will the FBI remove my record?**
A: Yes. FDLE forwards the court order to the FBI, and the FBI updates its records accordingly. This can take several weeks to months.

**Q: Can I verify that my record has been sealed or expunged?**
A: Yes. You can contact FDLE to request verification, or you can run a background check on yourself to confirm the record no longer appears.

**Q: What if my record still shows up after the order is granted?**
A: Contact your attorney immediately. They can follow up with the agencies that have not complied and take additional legal action if necessary.
