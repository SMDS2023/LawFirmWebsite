### The Three Types of Florida Citations

*Focus: overview*

**Key Points:**
- Civil traffic infractions (most common - speeding, red lights, etc.)
- Civil infractions with mandatory court appearance (30+ over, school zones)
- Notice to Appear (NTA) - criminal misdemeanor citations

**The Critical Distinction:**
| Type | Example | Criminal? | Creates Record? | Right to PD? |
|------|---------|-----------|-----------------|--------------|
| Civil Infraction | Speeding 15 over | NO | NO | NO |
| Civil + Mandatory Court | Speeding 30+ over | NO | NO | NO |
| Notice to Appear (NTA) | Petit theft, trespass | YES | YES | YES |


### Civil Traffic Infractions - NOT Criminal

*Focus: civil_infractions*

**Key Points:**
- Definition from Florida Statutes Ch. 316, 318, 320, 322
- No jail time possible
- No right to jury trial
- No right to court-appointed attorney
- No criminal record - ever
- Penalties: fines, points, traffic school, administrative license suspension

**Source:** Florida CJI Training (p. 420): "A driver with a civil traffic infraction does not face incarceration; they have no right to a trial by jury or court-appointed lawyer."


### Mandatory Court Appearance Does NOT Mean Criminal

*Focus: mandatory_court*

**Key Points:**
- 30+ mph over the speed limit requires court - still CIVIL
- School zone and construction zone violations - still CIVIL
- Certain crash-related citations - still CIVIL
- You appear in CIVIL traffic court, not criminal court
- No public defender because it's not criminal
- Still no criminal record regardless of outcome

**Why This Confuses People:**
- "Mandatory court" sounds serious and scary
- The courthouse is the same building
- The process feels similar
- But the legal consequences are completely different

**What Actually Happens:**
- You appear before a hearing officer or judge
- You can contest the ticket or negotiate
- Worst case: fines, points, traffic school
- You walk out - no arrest, no criminal record


### The Notice to Appear (NTA) - This IS an Arrest

*Focus: nta_trap*

**Key Points:**
- An NTA is a criminal arrest - you were just released instead of jailed
- The signature is your promise to appear (not admission of guilt)
- You now have a criminal case pending
- Right to public defender (because it's criminal)
- Right to jury trial
- Potential jail time if convicted
- Criminal record implications

**Common NTA Offenses:**
- Petit theft / shoplifting
- Trespassing
- Disorderly conduct
- Possession of drug paraphernalia
- Minor in possession
- Many first-time misdemeanors

**The Trap:**
Many people receive an NTA and think "I just got a ticket" because:
- It looks like a ticket
- The officer didn't handcuff them
- They drove away
- No booking, no mugshot

But legally, you WERE arrested. The NTA is simply a release mechanism that substitutes for taking you to jail.


### Why This Distinction Matters - Real Consequences

*Focus: consequences*

**Key Points:**
- Employment applications: "Have you ever been arrested?" - NTA = YES
- Background checks show the arrest
- Failure to appear consequences differ dramatically:
  - Civil: license suspension, additional fines
  - Criminal (NTA): warrant for arrest, additional criminal charge (FTA)
- Ignoring an NTA can result in being arrested at a future traffic stop

**Story Example:**
Client receives NTA for shoplifting, thinks it's "just a ticket," doesn't show up to court. Warrant issued. Six months later, pulled over for broken taillight - arrested on the spot, taken to jail, now facing original charge PLUS failure to appear.


### What To Do If You Receive Each Type

*Focus: action_steps*

**Civil Infraction (No Mandatory Court):**
- Pay the fine online, OR
- Elect traffic school (if eligible), OR
- Contest in court

**Civil Infraction (Mandatory Court):**
- You MUST appear - cannot pay online
- Consider hiring an attorney for point reduction
- Still not criminal - don't panic

**Notice to Appear (NTA):**
- This is a CRIMINAL CASE - treat it seriously
- Contact a criminal defense attorney immediately
- Do NOT miss your court date
- Do NOT discuss the case with anyone
- Preserve any evidence in your favor
