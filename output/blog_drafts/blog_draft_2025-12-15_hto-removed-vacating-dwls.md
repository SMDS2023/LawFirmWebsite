## The Story

### The Situation

Client came to us with a Habitual Traffic Offender (HTO) designation - a 5-year license revocation. The HTO was based on three DWLS (Driving While License Suspended) convictions within five years.

**The Problem:** He didn't have an attorney on any of the original DWLS cases. He just pled out, took the conviction, moved on. After the third one, DHSMV designated him HTO.

**What Most People Think:** Wait 12 months, apply for hardship, hope for the best. Drive legally (maybe) for the remaining 4 years with heavy restrictions.

**What We Did Instead:** We looked at the underlying convictions.

## Content Outline

### H2: What Is HTO and Why It's Not Always Permanent

**Key Points:**
- HTO requires 3+ qualifying offenses within 5 years (F.S. 322.264)
- DWLS convictions are qualifying offenses
- Standard approach: wait 12 months, petition for hardship
- BUT: HTO is only as solid as the convictions supporting it
- Remove one conviction → only 2 qualifying offenses → no more HTO

---

### H2: The Strategy - Challenge the Underlying Conviction

**Key Points:**
- Client didn't have attorney on original DWLS cases
- We reviewed the case files for all three convictions
- Identified opportunity to challenge one conviction
- Had client complete a driving improvement course
- Filed motion asking judge to reconsider the conviction
- Judge granted the motion → conviction vacated

**Why This Worked:**
- No attorney on original case = potential issues not raised
- Driving class showed rehabilitation/good faith
- Motion to reconsider gave judge a path to fix the situation

---

### H2: The Outcome - HTO Gone in Weeks

**Key Points:**
- One DWLS conviction vacated
- Only 2 qualifying offenses remaining
- No longer meets HTO threshold under F.S. 322.264
- Filed paperwork with DHSMV
- HTO designation removed
- Client eligible for full license reinstatement (not just hardship)

**Timeline:** About 3-4 weeks from filing motion to resolution

**Note:** There's sometimes a short delay after court approval while DHSMV updates their records. This is normal.

**Client Reaction:** He was thrilled to drive legally again without constantly looking over his shoulder.

---

### H2: Important Warning - Don't Drive During Suspension

**Key Points:**
- While fighting HTO, you may be tempted to drive
- Getting caught driving during suspension creates serious complications
- New DWLS/DWLSR charge makes everything harder
- If you're in the 12-month "hard suspension" period for HTO hardship, driving can destroy your petition
- DHSMV hearing officers have discretion - a violation during suspension gives them reason to deny
- The short-term convenience isn't worth the long-term consequences

**Bottom Line:** If you're fighting to get your license back, don't give them more ammunition. Find another way - rides, Uber, bus, whatever it takes.

---

### H2: Is This Strategy Right for Your HTO Case?

**Key Points:**
- Works best when underlying convictions have issues:
  - No attorney on original case
  - Constitutional problems with the stop
  - Notice/procedural defects
  - Clerical errors
- Harder when convictions are solid (especially felonies like FATE or DUI)
- Every case is different - need to review the actual case files
- Not just your driving record - the court files for each conviction

**What We Look For:**
- Did you have an attorney?
- Were there any issues with how you were stopped?
- Did you receive proper notice of suspension?
- Any procedural irregularities?
