Arrested Over Labor Day? Florida Deadlines Run

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | September 5, 2026
Criminal Defense DUI Holiday Arrests

A Labor Day weekend arrest means booking into jail while the courthouse next door is closed. A Labor Day weekend arrest triggers a first appearance within 24 hours, and a DUI arrest starts a 10-day license deadline that counts the holiday. Clerk offices are closed through Monday, September 7, 2026, but those clocks keep running.

What is closed over Labor Day weekend 2026?

Labor Day falls on Monday, September 7. The Orange County Clerk of Courts lists that Monday as a holiday closure, and the Ninth Circuit's court-holiday calendar matches. Add the weekend and the Clerk is dark from Saturday, September 5 until Tuesday, September 8.

Closed offices mean no new filings, no records requests, and no regular court divisions. They do not mean the process pauses. The jail keeps booking, a judge holds one session a day, and the state's license agency keeps counting.

Expect a busy jail. The City of Tampa's August 31 release ties Florida agencies to the national Drive Sober or Get Pulled Over campaign, which runs through Labor Day itself. WFTV reported an Orlando police DUI detail with nearly 100 officers over July 4, and holiday saturation patrols repeat.

Our post on Labor Day traffic stops in Florida covers the stop itself. This post covers what happens after the handcuffs go on.

Does first appearance still happen on a holiday?

Yes. Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.130 requires every arrested person to go before a judge within 24 hours, in person or by video. That rule counts every hour, and weekends and holidays do not extend it.

Orange County holds two weekday sessions, at 9 a.m. and 1 p.m. On weekends and court holidays there is one session, around 11 a.m., at the Booking and Release Center with no public gallery.

The Ninth Circuit's holiday order sets a 3 a.m. booking cutoff. Book after that and you wait for the next day's session, which is how a Sunday night arrest lands at the Labor Day Monday hearing.

At that hearing the judge confirms probable cause and sets bond or release conditions. The judge can also appoint a lawyer if waiting for hired counsel would keep you jailed past 24 hours. Our guide to your first court appearance in Orlando walks through the hearing itself.

A late first appearance does not void the case

Florida gives no dismissal remedy for a delayed hearing. Rule 3.133(a) requires a probable-cause finding within 48 hours of a warrantless arrest, and without one you must be released on your own recognizance. The remedy is release, not dismissal, and the charge continues.

Can you bond out before you see a judge?

Often, yes. F.S. 903.011 sets a statewide bond schedule, and the Ninth Circuit's order lets jail staff release you on that schedule before first appearance. Schedule bonds post around the clock at the Booking and Release Center, holiday or not. Out-of-state residents post double the schedule amount.

A first simple DUI carries a schedule bond, but the DUI statute holds you first. You cannot be released until you are no longer impaired, your alcohol level is below .05, or 8 hours have passed since arrest, whichever comes first.

Who waits for the judge regardless of bond amount

  • Domestic violence arrests, which state law holds until a judge sets bail. See our post on holiday domestic violence arrests.
  • Capital and life felonies, and first-degree felonies punishable by life.
  • Anyone on probation or pretrial release when arrested, registered sex offenders, and injunction violators.
  • Second-degree felonies and above, plus listed violent, trafficking, and racketeering offenses.

A no-bond hold is different from waiting for a session. On capital or life felonies the State must prove guilt is evident or the presumption great before bail is denied, under State v. Arthur. That fight happens at a hearing after first appearance, not at the jail.

How does the DUI 10-day license clock work?

The DUI citation is also your notice of suspension and a 10-day driving permit. F.S. 322.2615 gives you 10 days from the date the notice is issued to request a formal, informal, or eligibility review. The notice is issued at the roadside, so the clock starts on the arrest date.

The Bureau of Administrative Reviews in Orlando is closed on the holiday. That closure does not stop the count. A holiday in the middle of the count, like this Labor Day, still counts as a day.

The Department's own rule, Florida Administrative Code 15A-6.004, moves a deadline only when day 10 itself lands on a weekend, holiday, or office-closure day. For every Labor Day weekend arrest date, day 10 is a regular business day, so no extension applies.

Day 10 for Labor Day weekend arrests

Arrested Friday, September 4: request by Monday, September 14. Saturday, September 5: by Tuesday, September 15. Sunday, September 6: by Wednesday, September 16. Monday, September 7: by Thursday, September 17. All four are business days, so nothing rolls forward.

This is a civil track that runs beside the criminal case. The Florida Supreme Court in Lescher treated license consequences as regulatory, not punishment, and they keep their own calendar.

A holiday even pauses the Department's own clock. A 2026 Twelfth Circuit appellate decision held its 7 working days to mail a decision exclude holidays. Your 10 days get no such break in the middle of the count.

The formal review hearing is the real fight. Florida courts, including the Supreme Court in Hernandez, let you challenge whether the arrest was lawful at that hearing, and later circuit-court review is narrow. A refusal since October 1, 2025 also adds a separate misdemeanor count under Florida's new DUI refusal law.

What does the closed courthouse actually change?

Less than most people expect. The State Attorney's charging decisions and arraignment scheduling resume Tuesday, September 8. A hearing set for a later date is not a paused deadline. The short custody and license clocks already ran over the weekend.
