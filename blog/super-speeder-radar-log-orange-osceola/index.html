Super Speeder Radar Thrown Out in Orange and Osceola

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | November 12, 2026
Super Speeder Traffic Crimes Florida Law

Can a judge throw out super speeder radar evidence because of a bad log? Two Central Florida judges just did. In Osceola County and Orange County, radar speed was kept out because the officer's log showed a pass mark instead of the test numbers.

Osceola County

"Radar check okay"

No internal or tuning fork numbers. The judge rejected the log.

Orange County

"P" in every box

The State asked the judge to reconsider. The judge kept the radar out.

Can a bad radar log get super speeder radar thrown out?

Yes, it can. Florida's super speeder law, F.S. 316.1922, covers driving 50 mph or more over the limit. It also covers 100 mph or more in a way that puts people or property at risk.

Most of these cases start with a radar number. If the judge won't let the jury hear that number, the State has to prove your speed some other way.

What checks does Florida require on a radar?

The rule is Florida Administrative Code Rule 15B-2.009. Before an officer uses radar on a shift, and again before the shift ends, two checks have to be done.

  • Internal check: the officer presses the test button. The check passes only if the exact numbers the manufacturer lists appear on the radar's display.
  • Tuning fork check: the officer strikes certified tuning forks in front of the antenna. The forks must be certified, accurate to within 1 mph, and meet the manufacturer's specifications.
  • Written log: the officer must keep a written log of each internal and tuning fork check. The agency can design its own log form.

A separate test happens at least every six months on a technician's bench. That certificate gives the State a presumption that the radar works, under F.S. 316.1905(3)(b). You can rebut it with competent evidence.

Separate from the log, F.S. 316.1906(2) keeps radar speed out unless the officer is trained on radar, makes an independent visual estimate of your speed, has the audio Doppler on, and uses an approved unit.

What happened in Osceola County?

The log said "radar check okay." It did not record the internal check numbers or the tuning fork readings. The judge found that wasn't enough and rejected the log.

What happened in Orange County?

The case involved a Florida Highway Patrol trooper. His log had columns for the internal check and for tuning fork tests in stationary and moving mode. Each box held the letter "P."

The fork ratings were printed once at the top of the page. Nothing showed which fork was used for which test, or what the radar actually read.

The judge excluded the radar speed. The State then asked the judge to reconsider. The judge said no.

What did the State argue?

  • The rule says the numbers must appear on the radar's display, not in the log.
  • The rule lets each agency design its own log form.
  • Writing the same numbers every shift would be redundant.
  • The defense can cross-examine the trooper to fill any gaps.

Why did the judges disagree?

The rule tells the officer what to test, what result counts as a pass, and that each check goes in a written log. A "P" records a conclusion. It does not record what the officer saw.

Without the numbers, there is nothing to cross-examine. A trooper can't remember what one fork read on one shift months ago. The Orange County judge said that if the FHP logs had more data, the ruling would have been different.

What does a radar log that holds up look like?

The form itself was never the problem. Agencies can lay out the page however they want. What matters is the data in the boxes.

At the Orange County hearing, the judge looked at a sheriff's deputy's log that recorded the tuning fork readings. The judge found that log substantially complied with the rule. The FHP logs did not come close.

What happens to your case if the radar is excluded?

The State may try to prove your speed another way, such as pacing. Whether that works depends on your facts. A ruling in another county is not binding on your judge.

The radar log is one of several records that can decide a super speeder case. For another way radar evidence fails, read why the State couldn't prove speed in a super speeder trial. For how super speeder compares to racing, see street racing vs. super speeder.

Records your attorney can request

  • The officer's daily radar log for the shift
  • The six-month bench test certificate for the radar unit
  • The tuning fork certifications
  • The officer's radar training records

How a Criminal Defense Attorney Can Help

A super speeder charge is criminal. A first conviction carries up to 30 days in jail, a $500 fine, or both. A second conviction carries up to 90 days, a $1,000 fine, or both. If it comes within five years of a prior conviction, your license is revoked for 180 days to one year.
