HTO Revocation Overturned, DWLS Charge Dropped

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | October 15, 2026
Case Result Habitual Traffic Offender License Suspension

One designation. Two cases. Both came down.

The Result

  • The administrative side: our client faced a five-year license revocation as a habitual traffic offender (HTO). The court amended two of her prior DWLS cases to a withhold of adjudication and reversed the license suspension.
  • The criminal side: she also faced a county-court misdemeanor charge of driving while license suspended (DWLS) under F.S. 322.34(2), carrying possible jail time. The State nolle prossed the charge.
  • Her license is valid again, and she has no pending case.

These were two separate matters on the same driving record: an administrative HTO revocation and a criminal DWLS charge in county court. We handled both.

What Is a Habitual Traffic Offender in Florida?

Under F.S. 322.264, a habitual traffic offender is a person whose driving record, as kept by the Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (DHSMV), shows enough convictions within a 5-year period. There are two ways to get there:

  • Three serious offenses: three or more convictions, arising out of separate acts, for offenses such as DUI under F.S. 316.193, driving while license suspended or revoked, a felony in which a motor vehicle is used, leaving the scene of a crash with death or injury, or driving without a valid license under F.S. 322.03.
  • Fifteen moving violations: fifteen convictions for moving traffic offenses for which points may be assessed under F.S. 322.27, including the serious offenses above.

The count runs off the DHSMV record. That record is only as accurate as the convictions reported to it.

How Does an HTO Revocation Happen?

Once the record shows the qualifying convictions, F.S. 322.27(5)(a) says the department "shall revoke the license of any person designated a habitual offender," and the person "is not eligible to be relicensed for a minimum of 5 years from the date of revocation, except as provided for in s. 322.271."

The revocation is administrative. It comes from the DHSMV, usually by mailed notice, not from a judge at sentencing. Many drivers learn about it only when they are stopped.

How Our Client's HTO Count Built Up

Our client's record did not show a pattern of dangerous driving. It showed three strikes of one kind: driving while license suspended, the offense listed in F.S. 322.264(1)(d).

  • Strike one, about five years ago: a DWLS citation, resolved as a conviction a few months later.
  • Strike two, about two years ago: a second DWLS citation, issued together with other traffic citations.
  • Strike three, about fifteen months ago: a third DWLS citation.
  • A few months ago: a new DWLS stop, filed as a criminal charge under F.S. 322.34(2). The State later nolle prossed it.

The two newer DWLS cases were resolved recently, with adjudication withheld. That put three DWLS entries on the record within 5 years.

Underneath the strikes were suspensions of a common kind: insurance (financial responsibility) suspensions and a suspension for an unpaid traffic fine. None of the three strikes was a DUI.

How Can an HTO Designation Be Challenged?

Florida law gives more than one path. Which one fits depends on the record.

  • Show cause to the department: F.S. 322.27(5)(a) provides that any person whose license is revoked "may, by petition to the department, show cause why his or her license should not be revoked."
  • Attack the qualifying convictions: the designation depends on each counted conviction. If a conviction should not be on the record, or was later set aside in court, the count can fall below the threshold.
  • Amended disposition for certain DWLS strikes: under F.S. 322.27(5)(b), when the revocation results from a third DWLS violation and the driver provides proof of compliance for an offense listed in F.S. 318.14(10)(a)1.-5., "the clerk of court shall submit an amended disposition to remove the habitual traffic offender designation."
  • Court review: under F.S. 322.27(7), review of a suspension or revocation order is by writ of certiorari as provided in F.S. 322.31.

In our client's case, the court granted a motion to amend two prior DWLS dispositions to a withhold of adjudication and directed the clerk to reverse the license suspension. The State also nolle prossed the criminal DWLS charge.

What If the Designation Stands? The Hardship License

A driver revoked as an HTO is not eligible for the ordinary hardship hearing under F.S. 322.271(1)(a). Instead, F.S. 322.271(1)(b) allows the driver, "upon expiration of 12 months from the date of such revocation," to petition the department for reinstatement on a restricted basis "solely for business or employment purposes." The department investigates the driver's qualification, fitness, and need to drive, and holds a hearing.

That is a restricted license after a year without driving. Removing the designation itself is a different result.

What Are the Penalties for Driving While License Suspended?

F.S. 322.34 grades the offense by knowledge and history:

  • Without knowledge: a moving violation under F.S. 322.34(1), punishable under chapter 318.
  • Knowing, first conviction: a misdemeanor of the second degree under F.S. 322.34(2)(a). Under F.S. 775.082(4)(b), that carries "a definite term of imprisonment not exceeding 60 days."
  • Knowing, second or later conviction: a misdemeanor of the first degree under F.S. 322.34(2)(b), up to 1 year under F.S. 775.082(4)(a).
  • Knowing, third or later: a minimum of 10 days in jail, and a third-degree felony in some cases tied to DUI, refusal, or fleeing.
  • Driving while designated an HTO: a felony of the third degree under F.S. 322.34(5), up to 5 years under F.S. 775.082(3)(e).

Knowledge can be shown by a prior citation, an admission, or the notice required on a suspending judgment or citation. Our client's charge was filed in county court as a second-degree misdemeanor under F.S. 322.34(2). With the charge nolle prossed, she faces none of these penalties.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is an HTO revocation in Florida?

A minimum of 5 years from the date of revocation under F.S. 322.27(5)(a), unless relief is granted under F.S. 322.271.

Can I get a hardship license as an HTO?

Possibly. After 12 months, F.S. 322.271(1)(b) allows a petition for a license restricted to business or employment purposes.

Do three DWLS tickets make me an HTO?

Three convictions for driving while suspended or revoked, from separate acts within 5 years, count under F.S. 322.264(1)(d). A withheld adjudication under F.S. 322.34(11) is not a conviction.

What does "nolle prossed" mean?

The State formally dropped the charge. There is no conviction and no sentence.

Is driving on an HTO revocation a felony?

Yes. Under F.S. 322.34(5), driving while designated an HTO is a third-degree felony.
