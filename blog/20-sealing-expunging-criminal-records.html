Understanding Sealing vs. Expungement

What is Sealing?

Sealing is defined under Florida Statute § 943.045(19) as "the preservation of a record under such circumstances that it is secure and inaccessible to any person not having a legal right of access to the record or the information contained and preserved therein."

Key Characteristics of Sealing:

  • The criminal record is hidden from public view but not destroyed
  • The record remains confidential and inaccessible to most employers, landlords, and the general public
  • Certain government agencies retain access under specific circumstances
  • Sealed records must be disclosed when purchasing firearms or applying for concealed carry permits
  • Available immediately upon case resolution or completion of court-ordered supervision

What is Expungement?

Expungement is defined under Florida Statute § 943.0585 as "the court-ordered physical destruction or obliteration of a record or portion of a record by any criminal justice agency having custody thereof."

Key Characteristics of Expungement:

  • Criminal records are physically destroyed or obliterated by all agencies
  • Provides the highest level of privacy protection
  • Only a confidential copy is retained by the Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE)
  • Expunged records do NOT need to be disclosed when purchasing firearms or applying for concealed carry permits
  • Generally available only after charges are dismissed, dropped, or result in acquittal (or after a record has been sealed for 10 years)

The Right to Deny Your Criminal History

Both sealing and expungement allow you to lawfully deny or fail to acknowledge the arrest or criminal record in most circumstances, including employment applications, housing applications, educational applications, and most professional licensing applications.

Important Exceptions - You MUST Disclose When:

  • Applying for employment with criminal justice agencies
  • Seeking certain professional licenses (teaching, law enforcement)
  • Applying to own or operate a care facility
  • Running for public office
  • Involved in subsequent criminal proceedings
  • Seeking to seal or expunge another record

Eligibility Requirements

General Eligibility Criteria

To qualify for either sealing or expungement in Florida, you must meet ALL of the following requirements:

1. One-Time Opportunity

Florida law allows you to seal or expunge only ONE criminal record in your lifetime. If you have previously sealed or expunged a record in Florida, you are automatically disqualified.

2. No Prior Convictions

You must never have been adjudicated guilty in Florida of any criminal offense. Out-of-state convictions generally do not disqualify you.

3. No Pending Charges

You cannot have any pending criminal charges or be under any form of court supervision (probation, community control) at the time of application.

4. Eligible Offense

Your offense must not be on Florida's list of disqualifying offenses (see below).

Specific Eligibility: Sealing

You may be eligible to have your record sealed if:

  • The court withheld adjudication after you entered a plea of guilty or no contest
  • You were never adjudicated guilty (formally convicted)
  • You have completed all terms of probation or court-ordered supervision
  • The offense is not on the list of ineligible offenses under § 943.0584
  • Sealing is available immediately upon completion of court-ordered supervision

Specific Eligibility: Expungement

You may be eligible to have your record expunged if:

  • Your charges were dismissed, dropped, or not prosecuted (nolle prosequi), OR
  • You were found not guilty at trial, OR
  • A previously sealed record has been sealed for at least 10 years

Exception: Even if adjudication was withheld or you were found not guilty, you cannot expunge until the record has been sealed for 10 years, UNLESS no plea was entered or charges were dismissed before trial.

Disqualifying Offenses (Per § 943.0584, F.S.)

The following offenses are NEVER eligible for sealing or expungement, even if adjudication was withheld:

Violent Crimes:

  • Murder, Manslaughter, Homicide
  • Aggravated Assault or Battery
  • Felony Battery
  • Kidnapping or False Imprisonment
  • Carjacking or Robbery (all forms)
  • Home-Invasion Robbery
  • Assault or Battery (domestic violence)
  • Domestic Battery by Strangulation

Sex Offenses:

  • Sexual Battery (all Chapter 794 offenses)
  • Lewd or Lascivious Offenses (involving persons under 16)
  • Sexual Performance by a Child
  • Computer Pornography
  • Any offense requiring sex offender registration

Crimes Against Children:

  • Child Abuse or Aggravated Child Abuse
  • Luring or Enticing a Child
  • Selling or Buying of Minors

Other Serious Offenses:

  • Arson
  • Burglary of a Dwelling
  • Drug Trafficking
  • Manufacturing Controlled Substances
  • Human Trafficking
  • Abuse of Elderly or Disabled Person

The Step-by-Step Process

Successfully sealing or expunging your criminal record involves multiple stages, each requiring careful attention to detail. Here's what to expect:

Phase 1

Eligibility Assessment (1-7 Days)

What Happens:

  • Review your complete criminal history
  • Identify the specific case(s) you wish to seal or expunge
  • Verify eligibility based on statutory requirements
  • Determine whether sealing or expungement is appropriate
  • Gather preliminary case information

With an Attorney: Many experienced expungement firms offer free eligibility reviews and can assess your case within minutes to days, ensuring you don't waste time and money pursuing relief you're not eligible for.

Phase 2

Certificate of Eligibility Application (2-4 Months)

What Happens:

The Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE) must issue a Certificate of Eligibility before you can petition the court. This is a mandatory first step.

Required Documents for ALL Applications:

  1. Complete FDLE Application Form (FDLE 40-021)
  2. Submit Fingerprints (taken by authorized law enforcement personnel)
  3. Provide Certified Disposition(s) from the Clerk of Court
  4. Pay FDLE Processing Fee ($75 - non-refundable)

For EXPUNGEMENT Applications ONLY:

Before submitting to FDLE, you must deliver the application to the State Attorney's Office in the county where you were charged. The State Attorney completes Section B, certifying whether the case is eligible for expungement. This review can take 2-6 weeks.

Processing Time: FDLE states that processing typically takes 12 weeks (90 business days) from the date a complete application is received. However, actual processing times can range from 2-6 months depending on current backlog and completeness of your application.

Important: The Certificate of Eligibility is valid for 12 months from the date of issuance.

Phase 3

Filing the Court Petition (1-4 Weeks)

What Happens:

Once you receive your Certificate of Eligibility from FDLE, you must file a formal petition with the court where the charges originated.

Required Documents:

  • Petition to Expunge or Seal (properly completed)
  • Sworn Affidavit (notarized)
  • Certificate of Eligibility from FDLE (original)
  • Proposed Order for the judge to sign

You must also pay the court filing fee (typically $42, varies by county) and submit copies to the State Attorney's Office, arresting agency, and FDLE.

Phase 4

State Attorney Review (2-6 Weeks)

What Happens:

After you file the petition with the court and serve copies on the State Attorney's Office, the prosecutor reviews your case and files a response.

Possible Outcomes:

No Objection (Most Common)

The State Attorney agrees with the petition. The judge typically signs the order without a hearing within 2-6 weeks.

Objection Filed (Rare)

The State Attorney opposes the petition. The court schedules a hearing where you must present evidence and legal arguments.

Phase 5

Court Order and Record Clearance (2-8 Weeks)

What Happens:

Once the judge signs the order to seal or expunge:

  1. Order Distribution (Immediate): Clerk of Court distributes certified copies to all relevant agencies
  2. Agency Compliance (Up to 60 Days): All agencies have up to 60 days to comply with the court order (most comply within 2-4 weeks)
  3. Verification: You can request verification from FDLE that the order has been processed

Sealing: Agencies mark records as sealed and restrict access

Expungement: Agencies physically destroy or obliterate records

Special Circumstances

Human Trafficking Victim Expungement

Florida Statute § 943.0583 allows victims of human trafficking to petition for expungement of criminal records resulting from offenses committed as part of the trafficking scheme.

Key Features:

  • Does NOT require a Certificate of Eligibility from FDLE
  • Entirely judicial proceeding (file directly with the court)
  • Available for offenses committed "as part of a human trafficking scheme"
  • Includes prostitution and related offenses (Chapters 796 and 847)
  • Conviction is deemed vacated due to substantive defect

Note: Does NOT apply to offenses listed in § 775.084(1)(b)1 (serious violent crimes)

Administrative Expungement

Florida Statute § 943.0581 allows for administrative expungement of arrests made "contrary to law or by mistake."

When Available:

  • Arrest was made in error (wrong person)
  • Arrest was made without legal basis
  • Arrest violated constitutional rights
  • Identity theft (someone else used your information)

Process: Apply directly to FDLE (not a court proceeding). No Certificate of Eligibility required.

Expungement After 10-Year Sealing

If your record has been sealed for at least 10 years, you may petition to expunge it, even if adjudication was withheld and you pled guilty or no contest.

Requirements:

  • Record has been sealed for 10+ consecutive years
  • No criminal violations during the 10-year period
  • Still meet all other eligibility requirements

Benefits of Upgrading to Expungement:

  • Higher level of privacy (record destroyed)
  • No firearm disclosure requirement
  • Greater peace of mind

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I seal or expunge multiple arrests or cases?

A: Florida law generally allows for only one criminal record to be sealed or expunged in a lifetime. However, if multiple arrests or charges stem from a "single arrest or incident of alleged criminal activity," they may be sealed or expunged together in one proceeding.

Q: Will sealing or expunging my record remove it from Google and background check websites?

A: The court order directs criminal justice agencies to seal or expunge the official record. However, it does not automatically remove information from private websites, mugshot sites, or news articles. Many expungement attorneys offer additional services to help remove mugshots and online records.

Q: How long is the Certificate of Eligibility valid?

A: The Certificate of Eligibility is valid for 12 months from the date of issuance. If you do not file your court petition within that time, you must apply for a new certificate (and pay the $75 fee again).

Q: Can I be denied even if I receive a Certificate of Eligibility?

A: Yes. The Certificate of Eligibility only means you meet the statutory requirements for sealing or expungement. The judge still has discretion to deny the petition based on the facts of your case or objections from the State Attorney.

Q: Will employers see my sealed or expunged record?

A: Most employers will not see sealed or expunged records on background checks. However, there are exceptions for certain employers (criminal justice agencies, schools, nursing homes, etc.) who may have access to sealed records.

Q: Can I legally say "no" when asked if I've ever been arrested?

A: Yes, in most situations. Florida law allows you to lawfully deny or fail to acknowledge the arrest covered by the sealed or expunged record. Important exceptions: You must disclose when applying for certain jobs (law enforcement, teaching) or licenses.

Q: I was placed on probation and adjudication was withheld. Am I eligible?

A: If adjudication was withheld and you successfully completed probation, you are likely eligible for sealing (not expungement, unless the record is sealed for 10 years first). The offense must not be on the list of disqualifying offenses.

Q: My case was dismissed. Do I qualify for expungement?

A: If your case was dismissed (nolle prosequi, dropped, or charges not filed) and you meet all other eligibility requirements, you likely qualify for expungement.

Q: Can I seal or expunge a DUI?

A: It depends on the outcome. If you were convicted of DUI, it cannot be sealed or expunged. However, if charges were reduced to reckless driving with adjudication withheld, you may be able to seal the reckless driving record. If DUI charges were dismissed entirely, you may be eligible for expungement.

Q: Do I need an attorney, or can I do it myself?

A: You are not legally required to hire an attorney. However, the process is complex and technical. Attorneys have a 99%+ success rate, while self-filed petitions often have a 60-70% success rate due to errors. Most people find that hiring an attorney is worth the investment.

Q: How long does it take for agencies to comply with the court order?

A: Agencies have up to 60 days to comply with a court order to seal or expunge records. Most agencies comply within 2-4 weeks.
