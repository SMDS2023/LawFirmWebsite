DUI Diversion in Orange County: Why the Tier You're Offered Changes the Answer

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | July 6, 2026
DUI Defense Diversion Programs

In late June, an email from the State Attorney's Office told me a client had been approved for DUI pre-trial diversion. Good news — that's the outcome we'd pushed for. But "approved" is where the real decision starts. Whether a DUI diversion offer is worth taking depends almost entirely on which tier you're offered — and on what the fine print demands from the next year of your life.

Why a Dismissal Is Worth So Much in a DUI Case

DUI pre-trial diversion in Orange County is a deferred-prosecution program: the State Attorney's Office pauses the prosecution while you complete a supervised program, and if you finish, the charge is dismissed. To understand why that dismissal matters more in a DUI case than in almost any other misdemeanor, you have to look at a statute most people have never heard of.

The Statute That Closes Every Other Door

Under F.S. §316.656, no court may suspend, defer, or withhold adjudication for a DUI conviction. In plain English: if you're convicted of DUI in Florida — by plea or by verdict — it is a conviction, forever. There is no "withhold" like other charges get. And the damage spreads: under Florida's sealing and expungement laws, once you've been adjudicated guilty of any criminal offense, you are no longer eligible to seal or expunge any record — ever. One DUI conviction doesn't just stay on your record; it locks your whole record open. The same statute also bars a judge from accepting a plea to a lesser offense if your breath or blood result was .15 or above. The law deliberately leaves very few clean exits from a DUI charge. Diversion is one of them. That's what makes the offer feel like a gift — and why the State can attach almost any conditions it wants.

The Two Tiers — and the Line at .15

The Ninth Circuit's DUI diversion program is tiered, and the tier assignment turns on the same .15 number the plea statute uses. Tier 1 is for cases with a breath result under .15 — supervision currently starting at 12 months, DUI school, an evaluation and any recommended treatment, community service, program costs, and restrictions on your vehicle. (The program's terms change regularly — another reason to have current eyes on any offer.) Demanding, but manageable for most people.

Tier 2 — a refusal or a breath result of .15 or higher — is a different animal. The supervision runs longer, the costs run higher, and under the program's current terms it comes with a condition that changes the whole calculus: six months of continuous alcohol monitoring. That means a SCRAM device — an ankle bracelet you wear around the clock.

The SCRAM Problem

A SCRAM bracelet samples your perspiration for alcohol every 30 minutes, day and night — the manufacturer calls it "a breathalyzer for the ankle." You pay for the privilege: by the manufacturer's own published figures, setup runs $50 to $100 and monitoring costs roughly $10 to $12 per day — $300 to $360 a month. Over the six months Tier 2 requires, that's roughly $1,800 to $2,200 in monitoring fees alone, stacked on top of the program's other costs.

The money is only half the problem. The device is strapped to your ankle at work, at the gym, at your kid's soccer game — every day for six months. And it is not infallible. Defense lawyers around the country have documented false alerts from environmental alcohol: hand sanitizer, lotions, cleaning products, spilled gasoline. The manufacturer says its confirmation process screens those out. Sometimes it does. But in a diversion program, an alleged violation doesn't get a jury — it gets a program termination decision. A disputed alert can put you right back on the trial docket, months of compliance and thousands of dollars later, in a worse position than when you started.

Read That Again

At Tier 2, the device you wear to prove your sobriety can itself generate the violation that ends your diversion. When I said the tier changes the answer, this is what I meant: for many clients, Tier 2's cost, intrusion, and violation risk make defending the case the sounder path — especially when the stop, the roadside exercises, or the breath test have real problems.

Even Tier 1 Is a Compliance Gauntlet

Don't mistake Tier 1 for easy. Watching several diversion matters move through the system in a single month, the pattern is unmistakable: the program runs on hard deadlines and no grace. Contracts arrive electronically with a report-by date to Community Corrections. Payments are money orders, with receipts uploaded to prove them. Extension requests are routinely denied unless you're at the very end of the program. Miss a step — a report date, a payment window, a document upload — and the deferred prosecution stops being deferred.

That's the trade you're making, and it's why I've written before about a client kicked out of diversion over a device violation: the program's promise is real, but so is its machinery. You are trading a triable case for a year of perfect compliance.

What Saying No Looks Like

Declining diversion doesn't mean choosing a conviction. It means the case stays on the docket, where every element of a DUI still has to be proven: the stop, the detention, the exercises, the breath test and the machine behind it. Some of those cases get dismissed or suppressed into dismissal; some get reduced to reckless driving; some go to trial. And if the evidence is strong, a negotiated resolution is still available — with the statutory minimums as the floor. The point isn't that fighting always wins. The point is that "take the program" is only the right answer when the program's terms beat your realistic alternatives — and at Tier 2, with a SCRAM on your ankle, they often don't.

The Decision Framework I Walk Clients Through

  • Which tier — and is it even the right tier? A breath result hovering near .15 comes from a machine with a documented history of problems. The number that sets your tier is challengeable.
  • Total cost of compliance. Program fees, DUI school, treatment, lost time — and at Tier 2, roughly two thousand dollars of ankle bracelet. Price the whole program, not the brochure.
  • How strong is the case against you? A shaky stop or a challengeable breath test changes the math entirely. Never accept a program without someone evaluating the evidence first.
  • What happens if you slip? A violation — even a disputed one — usually means termination and prosecution, after you've already paid and complied for months.
  • Your license, your job, your record. CDL holders, professional licensees, and anyone whose career runs on a clean record need individual analysis before signing anything.

Bottom Line

Florida law slams almost every clean exit from a DUI charge: mandatory adjudication, no withhold, no seal, no plea below the charge at .15 and up. Diversion is the rare door the State leaves open, and for the right client at Tier 1, it can be exactly the right call. But the tier is everything. When the offer says Tier 2 — longer supervision, higher costs, and a SCRAM bracelet with a false-positive problem deciding your fate every 30 minutes — the gift needs a much harder look. That June email wasn't the end of my client's decision. It was the beginning of it.
