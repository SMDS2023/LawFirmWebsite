OCSO Is Ticketing Left-Lane Camping. The Statute Is Narrower.

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | August 31, 2026
Traffic Left Lane Law Orange County

Orange County deputies are citing drivers for staying in the left lane. Florida Statute 316.081(3) is about being overtaken, not a total ban.

This month the Orange County Sheriff's Office released body-camera video of deputies stopping drivers they call left-lane campers. ClickOrlando reported the release on August 25, 2026. In the footage, one deputy tells a driver the left-most lane is "for passing only." Another line from the stop: you are only allowed to come into that lane, pass a car, and then get out of it.

OCSO's release said most people don't realize that OCSO deputies enforce Florida's Left Lane Law. Cruising in the left lane, they say, is not just frustrating. It is dangerous for everyone. The video shows citations. One ticket is over $160. A related clip in the same reporting shows a sheriff's office vehicle flashing lights at a left-lane driver.

That is Orange County, this month. I practice criminal defense in Orlando. The legal question is narrower than the deputy's phrasing.

The statute is not a total ban

Florida Statute 316.081 is titled "Driving on right side of roadway; exceptions." Subsection (3) is the rule OCSO is pointing to. This is the 2025 Florida Statutes text, and it is still current:

On a road, street, or highway having two or more lanes allowing movement in the same direction, a driver may not continue to operate a motor vehicle in the furthermost left-hand lane if the driver knows or reasonably should know that he or she is being overtaken in that lane from the rear by a motor vehicle traveling at a higher rate of speed. This subsection does not apply to drivers operating a vehicle that is overtaking another vehicle proceeding in the same direction, or is preparing for a left turn at an intersection.

The trigger is being overtaken. The driver has to know, or reasonably should know, that a faster vehicle is coming up from behind in that same left-most lane. It is not a rule that the left lane is closed unless you are passing.

A deputy saying "for passing only" is agency shorthand. It is a field phrase. It is not the statute.

What 316.081(3) requires

The subsection applies on a road, street, or highway with two or more lanes going the same way. The restricted lane is the furthermost left-hand lane. The prohibition is continuing to operate there once you know, or reasonably should know, that you are being overtaken from the rear by a faster motor vehicle.

Two exceptions sit in the same subsection:

  1. You are overtaking another vehicle going the same direction.
  2. You are preparing for a left turn at an intersection.

Those are the two. The statute does not add a speed-limit floor. It does not limit the rule to roads posted 65 mph or higher.

Keep-right still sits around that subsection. 316.081(1) says drive on the right half of the roadway, with listed exceptions. 316.081(2) says a slower vehicle should use the right-hand lane then available, unless overtaking or preparing for a left turn at an intersection or into a private road or driveway. ClickOrlando summarized that older idea as drive in the right lane and pass on the left. Subsection (3) is the specific left-lane rule in the video.

Three Different Tickets, One Lane

There are a few ways a driver can be cited for being in the left lane. One statute is explicit. One is the speeding statute. One is the keep-right rule for a slower vehicle. Florida also has a statute titled obstruction. They are different tickets. They are not one rule.

The explicit statute is Florida Statute 316.081(3). That is the OCSO video statute. A driver may not continue in the furthermost left-hand lane if the driver knows, or reasonably should know, that he or she is being overtaken from the rear by a faster vehicle. The exceptions are overtaking, or preparing for a left turn at an intersection.

The speeding statute is Florida Statute 316.183, Unlawful speed. Subsection (1) says no driving faster than is reasonable and prudent. Subsection (5) says no driving at such a slow speed as to impede or block the normal and reasonable movement of traffic, except when reduced speed is necessary for safe operation or in compliance with law. Subsection (7) makes a violation a noncriminal moving violation under chapter 318. The same speeding statute can be written for going too fast in that lane, or for going so slow that you impede.

The keep-right rule is 316.081(2). A vehicle proceeding at less than the normal speed of traffic shall be driven in the right-hand lane then available, except when overtaking or passing, or preparing for a left turn at an intersection or into a private road or driveway. That is the closer vehicle-"in the way" rule. It is not the same as subsection (3).

316.2045 is titled Obstruction of public streets, highways, and roads. A person may not willfully obstruct the free, convenient, and normal use of a public street by impeding or hindering traffic, standing or remaining in the street, or endangering the safe movement of vehicles. A violation of (1)(a) is cited as a pedestrian violation under chapter 318. That is the obstruction statute by name. It is a willful-block, pedestrian-style cite. It is not the usual left-lane camping ticket. I have not seen a source that says OCSO is writing 316.2045 on the stops in that video.

The 2024 bill did not become law

ClickOrlando also covers House Bill 317 from 2024. That bill is easy to mix up with the current statute. Do not mix them up.

HB 317 would have required the left lane to be used to pass on roads, streets, or highways with at least two lanes and a posted speed limit of 65 mph. If Governor Ron DeSantis had signed it, it would have taken effect January 1, 2025. He vetoed it.

His veto message said the language was too broad. Drivers could have been ticketed for being in the furthest left lane even if they were not impeding traffic, or if there were few or no other cars nearby. He also said the bill could increase congestion in urban areas if drivers avoided the left lane for fear of a ticket. The proposed fine on that bill was up to $158.

That bill is not current law. The 65-and-over "left lane for passing only" ban did not pass. What is current is 316.081(3).

A citation is still a stop

316.081(5) says a violation of the section is a noncriminal traffic infraction, punishable as a moving violation as provided in chapter 318. It is not a crime. It is still a moving-violation stop.

The ClickOrlando piece shows citations on the video. One ticket is over $160. The same story says drivers found left-lane camping and impeding traffic can be fined over $150. I am not going to invent a points schedule, an insurance consequence, or a court flowchart that is not in those sources.

A moving-violation stop is more than the dollar line on the citation. It is a traffic stop. It is a record of the stop. What happens after that depends on the citation and the court.

The text to read after an Orange County stop on this statute is 316.081(3). The questions it asks are whether the driver knew, or reasonably should have known, that he or she was being overtaken, and whether the driver was overtaking or preparing for a left turn.
