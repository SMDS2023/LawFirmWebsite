Street Racing Revokes on a First Offense. Super Speeder Does Not.

Those two charges are not the same crime. One is F.S. 316.191. One is F.S. 316.1922. Speed can support both. Only racing takes the license on a first conviction.

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | September 3, 2026
Street Racing Super Speeder 316.191 316.1922

Ocoee police charged a driver with both. ClickOrlando reported on September 1, 2026, that officers clocked 100 mph on a stretch of Colonial Drive posted 45, around 3 a.m. on July 19. The story says the stop was posted as video. The charges, as Ocoee described them, were street racing and a Super Speeder / HB 351 violation.

I am not going to name anyone. I did not try that case. I practice criminal defense in Orlando. The reporting is the peg. The statutes are the point.

Those two charges are not the same crime. One is F.S. 316.191. One is F.S. 316.1922. Speed can support both. Only racing takes the license on a first conviction. Super Speeder, on a first conviction, does not.

I have already written Super Speeder case files: the News 13 interview, the Richardson hearing, and a radar-foundation dismissal. Those are results in Super Speeder files. This post is the compare.

Super Speeder is a speed number

This is the 2025 text of 316.1922, still current. A person commits dangerous excessive speeding if he or she operates a motor vehicle:

(a) In excess of the speed limit by 50 mph or more.

(b) At 100 mph or more in a manner that threatens the safety of other persons or property or interferes with the operation of any vehicle.

A hundred in a 45 is 55 over. That is (a). It is also 100 mph, so (b) is in play if the State can show the “in a manner that threatens” part.

Punishment on a first conviction is imprisonment for up to 30 days or a fine of $500, or both. There is no license revocation in that first-conviction paragraph.

A second or subsequent conviction is up to 90 days or a $1,000 fine, or both. If that second or subsequent violation is within 5 years of a prior 316.1922 conviction, the driving privilege is revoked for at least 180 days but no more than 1 year.

HB 351 is how the legislature got here. The crime on the books is 316.1922.

Street racing is a race, a stunt, or an exhibition of speed

316.191 is titled “Racing on highways, street takeovers, and stunt driving.” It is not a miles-over chart.

Subsection (2)(a) says a person may not drive any motor vehicle in any street takeover, stunt driving, race, speed competition or contest, drag race or acceleration contest, test of physical endurance, or exhibition of speed or acceleration, or for the purpose of making a speed record, on any highway, roadway, or parking lot.

“Race” in 316.191(1)(h) can be one or more motor vehicles. It can be a prior arrangement. It can also be an immediate competitive response to a challenge. The statute also reaches passengers, people who coordinate or collect money, people who block traffic for the event, people who film it (news media excepted), and people who haul fuel for it.

A first violation of subsection (2) is a first-degree misdemeanor. The fine is not less than $500 and not more than $2,000, and the department shall revoke the driver license of a person so convicted for 1 year. A hearing may be requested under 322.271.

That “shall revoke … for 1 year” is the first-offense license stake. Super Speeder’s first-conviction paragraph does not have one.

The racing statute also lets an officer arrest without a warrant on probable cause, and it lets a vehicle be impounded for 30 business days after that arrest.

DHSMV can move before a conviction

Conviction is not always when the license actually disappears.

F.S. 322.27(1)(a) says the department may suspend the license or identification card of any person without a preliminary hearing upon a showing of its records or other sufficient evidence that the licensee has committed an offense for which mandatory revocation of license is required upon conviction.

We ran into that on a hit-and-run file. F.S. 322.26(4) is the mandatory-revocation list item for failure to stop and render aid in a crash that results in death or personal injury. 316.027 is the leave-scene crime. 322.27(1)(a) is how DHSMV can suspend on the charge, before anyone is convicted.

Street racing, on conviction, requires a one-year revocation in 316.191 itself. A first Super Speeder conviction under 316.1922 does not. That is why charging both is not “two labels for the same speeding ticket.” One of them is built as a revoke-on-conviction offense. One of them is not, the first time.

The Ocoee numbers, without mixing the statutes

100 in a 45 can be Super Speeder because of the 50-over / 100-mph lines in 316.1922. The same stop can also be charged as 316.191 if the State’s theory is a race, a stunt, or an exhibition of speed. ClickOrlando says Ocoee charged both.

If the only proof is a speed, Super Speeder is the statute that is written as a speed. Racing still has to be racing, or stunt driving, or an exhibition of speed, as 316.191 defines those things.
