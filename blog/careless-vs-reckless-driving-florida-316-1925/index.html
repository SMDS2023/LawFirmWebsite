Careless vs Reckless Driving in Florida

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | September 25, 2026
Traffic Offenses Reckless Driving Careless Driving

Careless vs reckless driving in Florida is the difference between a civil ticket and a criminal charge. The line between them is the driver's mental state, not the maneuver itself. Careless driving falls under F.S. 316.1925, and reckless driving falls under F.S. 316.192. Neither statute changed in 2026.

Can the Same Driving Lead to Either Charge?

Yes. A fast lane change, a stretch of tailgating, or a near-miss can be written up either way. The officer decides at the roadside which box to check on the citation.

That choice sets the path of your case. A careless driving ticket is a civil matter you can pay, contest, or resolve with traffic school. A reckless driving charge opens a criminal case in county court. For an Orlando stop, that usually means Orange County Court.

Enforcement campaigns produce many of these stops. According to FDOT, Florida joined the multi-state Operation Southern Slow Down speed campaign on July 13-18, 2026. FDOT, FLHSMV, and the Florida Highway Patrol all took part.

Most of these stops still end in civil tickets. The South Florida Times reported that Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office aggressive-driving details produced 2,545 citations but only 9 arrests. If you are already on the criminal side, read what to expect with a first-time reckless driving charge in Florida.

What Is Careless Driving Under F.S. 316.1925?

The statute requires you to drive in a careful and prudent manner. That means accounting for the road's width, grade, and curves, plus traffic and other conditions. Falling short of that is careless driving.

It is a noncriminal moving violation. There is no jail and no criminal record. You face a civil fine that varies by county because of added court costs, plus 3 points on your license.

You have 30 days to pay, set up a payment plan, or ask for a hearing. If you choose a hearing, here is what happens at your first traffic court hearing in Florida.

A Serious Crash Changes a Careless Ticket

If a crash causes death or serious bodily injury, a court hearing is mandatory. You cannot simply pay the ticket, and the penalties are higher. Learn more about careless driving in Florida after a crash.

Is Reckless Driving a Crime in Florida?

Yes. F.S. 316.192 makes it a crime to drive in willful or wanton disregard for the safety of persons or property. Fleeing police in a vehicle counts as reckless driving automatically.

In plain terms, "willful or wanton" means the driver knew the danger and chose to ignore it. Florida courts describe it as conscious and intentional indifference to consequences. Carelessness or ordinary negligence is not enough.

That mental-state element is what makes reckless driving a crime. The state must prove it beyond a reasonable doubt, the same as any other criminal charge.

Where Do Officers and Prosecutors Draw the Line?

They look for speed plus something else. Common aggravators include weaving through dense traffic, near-misses with pedestrians, running signals, racing, and impairment.

Speed alone usually is not enough. In Luzardo v. State (Fla. 3d DCA 2014), driving about 84 mph in a 55 zone on a straight, clear road was careless, even negligent, but not reckless.

In Damoah v. State (Fla. 4th DCA 2016), the court separated negligent driving from criminal recklessness. Convictions tend to hold up when speed combines with weaving, heavy traffic, or impairment. Courts decide each case on its own facts.

Citation or Notice to Appear?

A careless driving ticket comes on a civil citation with a payment option. A reckless charge usually comes on a citation marked criminal, or a Notice to Appear, with a mandatory court date. Serious cases may end in a custodial arrest.

For more on that difference, see our guide to a Notice to Appear vs a regular traffic ticket.

How Does a Reckless Charge Become Careless?

Not through a jury verdict. In C.D. v. State (Fla. 3d DCA 1978), the court held that careless driving is not a lesser included offense of reckless driving. A jury cannot simply convict you of the smaller charge instead.

A reduction happens when the prosecutor amends the charge or the case resolves by agreement. That usually follows a defense challenge to the willful-or-wanton proof. Whether it happens depends on the facts, not a standard discount.

Careless vs Reckless Driving Penalties Side by Side

Careless driving carries a civil fine and 3 points. Reckless driving carries 4 points. A first reckless conviction can also mean up to 90 days in jail and a $25 to $500 fine.

Reckless driving penalties climb from there under F.S. 316.192:

  • Second or later conviction: Up to 6 months in jail and a $50 to $1,000 fine.
  • Property damage or injury: The charge becomes a first-degree misdemeanor.
  • Serious bodily injury: The charge becomes a third-degree felony.
  • Alcohol or drugs involved: The court may order DUI school.

Points also add up. Twelve points in 12 months can bring a suspension of up to 30 days. Eighteen in 18 months can mean up to 3 months, and 24 in 36 months up to a year.

Driving after a points suspension creates a separate problem, covered in our post on DWLSR vs no valid license in Florida.

A Reckless Conviction Is a Criminal Record

A careless driving ticket does not create a criminal record. A reckless driving conviction does, and that record may appear on background checks run by employers and landlords.

Which Charge Is on Your Paperwork?

Trust the citation over what was said at the roadside. An officer may call it "just a ticket" when the paperwork says criminal. The document controls which process applies to you.

Check Your Paperwork For

  • The offense description: careless driving or reckless driving
  • Whether it is marked as a civil infraction or a criminal violation
  • Whether it offers a payment option or orders you to appear in court
  • The court date, time, and courthouse, if one is listed
  • Your deadline to respond, which is 30 days for a civil ticket

Under F.S. 318.14, you can elect traffic school for a civil careless ticket. Adjudication is withheld, no points are assessed, and the civil penalty drops 18 percent.

You can use that option only once in 12 months, and there is a lifetime cap. It is not available for a criminal reckless driving charge.
