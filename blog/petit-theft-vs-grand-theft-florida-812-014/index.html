Petit vs Grand Theft in Florida: The $750 Line

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | September 28, 2026
Theft Charges Shoplifting Felony vs. Misdemeanor

Petit theft versus grand theft in Florida is a question of dollar value. Property worth $750 or more is grand theft, a felony, and anything less is petit theft, a misdemeanor. The number on the store's report decides which court you land in, and that number can be challenged.

Value is not the only path to a felony, though. Certain property, certain places, and a prior theft record can each make a small theft a felony.

Where do the dollar lines fall under F.S. 812.014?

The statute grades theft by the value of the property taken.

The theft tiers at a glance

  • Under $100: second-degree misdemeanor petit theft, up to 60 days in jail and a $500 fine.
  • $100 to under $750: first-degree misdemeanor petit theft, up to one year in jail and a $1,000 fine.
  • $750 to under $20,000: third-degree felony grand theft, up to five years in prison and a $5,000 fine.
  • $20,000 to under $100,000: second-degree felony grand theft, up to 15 years in prison.
  • $100,000 or more: first-degree felony grand theft, up to 30 years in prison.

The line that matters most is $750. The State has five years to file on either side of it, a trap covered in the five-year retail theft statute of limitations.

How is the value of stolen property decided?

Under F.S. 812.012, value means market value at the time and place of the offense. If that cannot be satisfactorily ascertained, it is replacement cost within a reasonable time after the offense.

If value cannot be ascertained, the judge or jury may find it was not less than a certain amount. If no minimum can be found, value is treated as less than $100. Separate thefts under one scheme or course of conduct may be added together to set the grade.

Where the store's number comes from

In Orange County, store staff usually detain you and call OPD or the Sheriff's Office. The officer charges from the store's item list, so every price on it matters.

Outside retail stores, value is often the central fight when a billing dispute becomes a felony in Florida.

Which items are grand theft at any price?

Under F.S. 812.014(2)(c), it is grand theft of the third degree, a third-degree felony, if the property stolen is any of the following, whatever its value:

  • A will, codicil, or other testamentary instrument.
  • A firearm. A second conviction for grand theft of a firearm is a second-degree felony under 812.014(2)(g).
  • A motor vehicle, except where a first-degree grand theft provision in 812.014(2)(a) applies.
  • Any commercially farmed animal, including any animal of the equine, avian, bovine, or swine class or other grazing animal. This also covers a registered beekeeper's bee colony and aquaculture species raised at a certified aquaculture facility. A $10,000 fine is imposed for these.
  • Any fire extinguisher installed in a building for fire prevention and control at the time of the taking. This does not apply to one taken from a point-of-sale business's inventory.
  • Citrus fruit of 2,000 or more individual pieces.
  • Property taken from a designated construction site identified by a posted sign under F.S. 810.09(2)(c).
  • Any stop sign.
  • Anhydrous ammonia.
  • Any amount of a controlled substance as defined in F.S. 893.02.

What other rules in F.S. 812.014 skip or lower the dollar line?

  • Property worth $40 to under $750 taken from a dwelling or its unenclosed curtilage is third-degree felony grand theft (812.014(2)(d)1.). Taking from more than 20 dwellings or their curtilage is a second-degree felony (812.014(2)(d)3.).
  • Property worth less than $40 taken from a dwelling or its unenclosed curtilage is first-degree misdemeanor petit theft. With a prior theft conviction, it becomes a third-degree felony (812.014(2)(e)).
  • Emergency medical equipment worth $300 or more from a licensed facility or permitted aircraft or vehicle is second-degree felony grand theft. So is law enforcement equipment worth $300 or more from an authorized emergency vehicle (812.014(2)(b)3.-4.).
  • Theft during a riot or aggravated riot, or in a county after the Governor declares a state of emergency, is graded higher when those conditions facilitate it. Second-degree grand theft becomes a first-degree felony. Third-degree grand theft of $5,000 to under $20,000 becomes a second-degree felony (812.014(2)(b) and (c)).
  • Any grand theft becomes a first-degree felony if the offender uses a motor vehicle as an instrumentality, not merely a getaway car, and damages another's real property. The same is true if the theft causes more than $1,000 in damage to another's real or personal property (812.014(2)(a)3.).
  • Coordinating one or more other people in thefts where the stolen property is worth more than $3,000 is a second-degree felony (812.014(6)).
  • Driving off without paying for gasoline dispensed into your tank is petit theft. A conviction carries a license suspension of up to 6 months for a first suspension and 1 year for a later one (812.014(5)).

Can a prior record make a small theft a felony?

Your record can move the line

Under F.S. 812.014(3), petit theft with one prior theft conviction is a first-degree misdemeanor. Petit theft with two or more prior theft convictions is a third-degree felony, whatever the value of the item.

That means a $20 item can be charged as a felony. A quick plea today can become the prior that raises the next charge.

When do several small trips become one felony?

F.S. 812.015, the retail theft statute, stacks on top of the general theft law. It lets the State add up separate retail thefts within 120 days. The window was 30 days until October 1, 2024.

  • $750 within 120 days: a third-degree felony when reached by coordinating others, planning to resell, or stealing from more than one location.
  • Three or more thefts of 10 or more items: a third-degree felony at two or more stores, regardless of value.
  • Second-degree felony: over $3,000 from coordinated or resale thefts, 20 or more items in three or more thefts at different stores, or a prior felony retail theft conviction.

Thefts in more than one judicial circuit go to the Statewide Prosecutor. A second or later petit theft conviction from a merchant also brings a mandatory $50 to $1,000 fine or court-ordered public service.

What else follows a theft charge?

Why did the store send me a letter demanding money?

Many retailers send a civil demand letter after a shoplifting stop, relying on F.S. 772.11. That statute allows three times actual damages, with a $200 minimum, plus attorney fees. A written demand must come first.

Paying within 30 days earns a written release for that act of theft. But the letter is not a court order, and paying it does not settle the criminal charge.

Which court and scoresheet apply?

Petit theft is heard in county court. Grand theft goes to circuit court with a felony sentencing scoresheet. Grand theft of $750 to $5,000 ranks at Level 2, while felony retail theft scores at Level 5 or 6.
