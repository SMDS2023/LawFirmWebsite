Florida's Dangerous Excessive Speeding Law Directs Attention to Orange County Speeding Data

Analyzing 46,851 speeding tickets to understand enforcement patterns as the new law takes effect

Published on January 30, 2025 by Attorney Jeff Lotter

New Criminal Penalties for Extreme Speeding Coming July 1, 2025

Florida dramatically changed how it handles extreme speeding violations. Starting July 1, 2025, drivers caught going 50+ mph over the speed limit or traveling at 100+ mph in a dangerous manner will face criminal charges, not just traffic tickets.

⚠️ New Criminal Thresholds:

  • 50+ mph over the speed limit
  • 100+ mph in a manner threatening safety

Penalties: Up to 30 days jail (first offense) or 90 days (second offense), plus fines and possible license revocation

2. Concentrated Enforcement Patterns

The data reveals significant concentration in enforcement:

Agency Concentration:

  • • Orange County Sheriff's Office: 18,675 tickets (40%)
  • • Florida Highway Patrol: 8,958 tickets (19%)
  • • Top 2 agencies: 59% of all tickets

Within these agencies, enforcement is further concentrated among specific officers. For example, in Winter Garden Police Department, Officer Bartko issued 1,067 tickets while Officer Mcmorrow issued only 76.

3. Age-Based Patterns

The dashboard clearly shows that speeding enforcement decreases with driver age:

  • Younger drivers (20s-30s) receive the highest number of citations
  • Citation frequency drops steadily with age
  • Drivers over 60 receive significantly fewer tickets

🎯 Defense Consideration:

These patterns could indicate selective enforcement or profiling that may be relevant to your defense strategy.

How the New Law Changes Everything

Under current law, even extreme speeding results in a civil traffic infraction. You pay a fine, possibly attend traffic school, and move on. But starting July 1, 2025:

Criminal Consequences:

Offense Penalty
First Offense Up to 30 days jail + $500 fine
Second Offense Up to 90 days jail + $1,000 fine + license revocation (180 days - 1 year)

How to Use This Data for Your Defense

Step 1: Research Your Officer

Use the dashboard above to find the officer who issued your ticket:

  1. Click on the agency that issued your ticket
  2. Look for your officer's name in the breakdown
  3. Note their ticket volume compared to other officers
  4. Screenshot any concerning patterns

Step 2: Identify Potential Issues

High-volume ticket writers may indicate:

  • Quota pressure: Unofficial pressure to write more tickets
  • Targeting patterns: Focus on specific areas or demographics
  • Procedural shortcuts: Less time spent on proper documentation
  • Equipment issues: Improper calibration or operation of speed devices

Step 3: Build Your Defense Strategy

Understanding these patterns helps build defenses based on:

  • Selective enforcement: Why you and not others?
  • Officer credibility: Volume-based enforcement vs. safety focus
  • Technical defenses: Radar/laser calibration and operation
  • Constitutional issues: Profiling or targeting concerns

Florida's new dangerous excessive speeding law represents a seismic shift in traffic enforcement. What was once a expensive ticket will soon carry the possibility of jail time and a criminal record.

The data from Orange County reveals clear patterns in current enforcement that will likely continue under the new law. By understanding these patterns—from agency concentration to officer-specific tendencies—drivers can better protect themselves and build stronger defenses when needed.
