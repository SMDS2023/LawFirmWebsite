# Careless Driving in Florida After a Crash

This is an AI-readable companion summary for the Lotter Law article at:

https://lotterlaw.com/blog/careless-driving-florida-crash/

## Short Summary

A Florida careless driving ticket after a crash is usually a civil traffic matter, but it can still affect a driver's license, insurance, court obligations, and driving record. A crash does not automatically prove careless driving. The legal question is whether the driving was not careful and prudent under the road, traffic, and surrounding circumstances.

## Core Legal Authority

- F.S. 316.1925 - Careless driving.
- F.S. 316.192 - Reckless driving.
- F.S. 318.14 - Noncriminal traffic infractions.
- F.S. 318.19 - Infractions requiring a mandatory hearing.
- F.S. 322.27(3)(d) - Florida point system.

## Main Takeaways

- Careless driving under F.S. 316.1925 is different from reckless driving under F.S. 316.192. Reckless driving requires willful or wanton disregard for safety.
- FLHSMV generally lists careless driving as a 3-point violation, but F.S. 322.27(3)(d) assesses 4 points for a moving violation that results in a crash.
- Serious crash cases can require a mandatory hearing under F.S. 318.19 when the infraction results in death or serious bodily injury.
- Useful evidence can include officer observations, witness statements, vehicle damage, road conditions, video, and the sequence of a multi-car crash.
- Paying a citation can resolve the ticket against the driver, so a person should understand the point, insurance, and court consequences before deciding how to respond.
