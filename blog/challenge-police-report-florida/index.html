Take Officers Off the Pedestal

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | October 30, 2026
Police Accountability Police Reports DUI Orange County

A badge is not proof of character. An officer's report is one person's account, and you have the right to challenge a police report in Florida like any other evidence. Florida law tells agencies to screen officers for good moral character. Screening only works when someone actually does it.

It's time society removes the pedestal we put officers on and lets them stand or fall on their own character. Most officers do the job well. Some should never have been hired. And few things are more dangerous than an intelligent and corrupt police officer.

What Happened in Downtown Orlando?

On October 17, 2026, Orlando police arrested an off-duty Haines City police officer who was driving a marked Haines City patrol car, outside his own jurisdiction. People had called in reckless driving downtown. ClickOrlando reported the car went the wrong way on one-way streets, with an open Four Loko in plain view.

Orange County court records show three cases. He is charged with DUI and reckless driving, and was cited for an open container. The record lists breath results of 0.266 and 0.265, more than three times the 0.08 limit. No plea has been entered. He is presumed innocent.

Haines City fired him the same day, FOX 35 reported.

Was This the First Time?

No. In June 2024 he reported for duty at the Polk County Sheriff's Office. A supervisor smelled alcohol, a DUI investigation followed, and his own agency arrested him for DUI before his shift, WWSB and WTSP reported. Deputies said he had driven his marked patrol car on duty.

He resigned. Prosecutors abandoned the charge in May 2025, and he was not convicted.

Haines City hired him in July 2026, about three months before the Orlando arrest. The chief said the department gave him “a second opportunity to serve in law enforcement.” The department told ClickOrlando he was not subject to random alcohol testing or any other special conditions.

A second chance can be fair. A second chance with no monitoring is a gamble with the public's safety. Were there signs the leadership missed? Did his fellow officers see anything that should have been flagged?

What Does Florida Law Require Before an Agency Hires an Officer?

Under F.S. 943.13(7), every officer must “have a good moral character as determined by a background investigation.” Under 943.13(8), the applicant signs a sworn affidavit. It must disclose “whether the applicant separated or resigned from previous criminal justice employment while he or she was under investigation.”

So the law already asks the right question. The hiring agency decides what to do with the answer. Nothing in 943.13 requires alcohol monitoring for a rehire.

After hiring, F.S. 943.1395(5) says an agency that suspects an officer no longer meets that standard “must conduct an internal investigation.” It then reports the findings to the state commission, which can suspend or revoke the officer's certification.

Is This Only About Drinking?

No. In August 2026, Haines City arrested a different officer. WESH reported an affidavit alleging 717 Flock license-plate searches for his estranged wife's car. Elsewhere, a Sumter County detective and a Sarasota officer were arrested over plate-camera searches this year. Each of them is presumed innocent.

The charges reported in these cases include official misconduct under F.S. 838.022, a third-degree felony.

What Happens When the Video and the Report Disagree?

The video can end the case. In Winter Springs, an officer arrested a driver at gunpoint outside his garage in 2024. The officer's report said the car “lost traction and fish-tailed.” The driver's Tesla camera told a different story.

At trial in July 2026, the judge acquitted the driver of reckless driving. In October, the chief announced the officer had left the department during an internal investigation. We covered that trial here.

How Do You Challenge a Police Report in Florida?

Police have extreme power and wide discretion. The job is stressful, and some officers cope by drinking. That is why an arrest report is a starting point, not the truth. If you are charged, your lawyer should test every line of it:

  • Every camera. Body-worn, in-car, and private video like a doorbell or a Tesla. Ask for it early, before it is overwritten.
  • The paper trail. The report, any sworn affidavit, the 911 call, and dispatch records. Times and locations have to line up.
  • The officer. Training and discipline records through a public-records request. A history of sustained complaints can matter.
  • Line by line. The report against the video. Every mismatch matters, and each one is a question for the officer under oath.

The officer's version is not the end of the story. The State still has to prove its case with evidence. When the evidence was gathered illegally, a motion to suppress can keep it out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you challenge a police report in Florida?

Yes. A police report is one officer's account, not proof. If you are charged, your lawyer can test it against body-worn and in-car video, private video, 911 and dispatch records, sworn affidavits, and the officer's training and discipline records.

Does Florida require background checks for police officers?

Yes. Under F.S. 943.13(7), every officer must have good moral character as determined by a background investigation. Under F.S. 943.13(8), the applicant's sworn affidavit must disclose whether the applicant separated or resigned from criminal justice employment while under investigation.

What happens when an officer no longer meets Florida's standards?

Under F.S. 943.1395(5), an agency that suspects an officer no longer meets the qualifications must conduct an internal investigation and report its findings to the Criminal Justice Standards and Training Commission, which can suspend or revoke certification.
