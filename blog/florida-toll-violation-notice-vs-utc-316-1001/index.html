Florida Toll Notice vs. UTC: What Statute 316.1001 Means

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | April 6, 2026
Traffic Violations Toll Violations License Suspension

Two very different pieces of paper can show up in your mailbox over the same missed toll. One is a bill you can pay and forget. The other is a Uniform Traffic Citation — a moving violation with points, a deadline, and a path to a suspended license. Confusing the two is how a $2 toll turns into a $500 court case. Here's how to tell which one you're holding.

How Florida's Toll Enforcement Works Under F.S. 316.1001

Under F.S. 316.1001, failing to pay a prescribed toll is a noncriminal traffic infraction punishable as a moving violation — but no one stops you. A camera photographs your plate, the first-listed registered owner becomes the Toll-by-Plate customer under Florida Administrative Code Rule 14-100.005, and everything afterward arrives by first-class mail to the address on your vehicle registration. A toll enforcement officer's written report plus the photographic evidence creates a rebuttable presumption that the registered owner committed the violation.

Florida's appellate courts made the structure explicit in the Tropical Trailer Leasing litigation: a Toll-by-Plate invoice is merely an assessment of tolls, and F.S. 316.1001's remedies apply only once an actual citation has been issued. The law recognizes two distinct stages — and your obligations are completely different at each one.

Stage One — The Toll Enforcement Notice: Just Pay the Bill

The first mailing is an invoice, not a ticket — on FDOT facilities, the unpaid toll plus a $2.50 administrative charge. It carries no points, creates no court case, and never touches your driving record. If unpaid, a second invoice follows after 20 days. Pay either one and the matter ends completely — no lawyer, no court, nothing to fight. Self-help is the whole answer at this stage.

Real Notice or Scam Text?

Legitimate Florida toll enforcement arrives by U.S. mail to the registered owner — never by a text demanding payment through a link. WTSP's VERIFY team reports an ongoing wave of fake "unpaid toll" texts impersonating SunPass and FLHSMV, with the FBI logging more than 2,000 complaints since March 2024; per that reporting, genuine SunPass texts come only from short code 786727. A text link pressuring you to pay a toll right now is a scam.

Stage Two — The Uniform Traffic Citation: Now It's a Moving Violation

Only after the second invoice goes unpaid may the tolling agency issue a Uniform Traffic Citation — one for each unpaid toll transaction, which is how a stack of toll citations can grow from a single unpaid account. A UTC changes everything:

  • It's a moving violation carrying points: Under F.S. 322.27, an adjudication adds 3 points to your driving record — one reason how you resolve the citation matters as much as whether you do.
  • The 30-day clock starts: Under F.S. 318.14, you have a 30-day election period to pay the civil penalty, elect a driver improvement course, or request a court hearing.
  • Contesting and losing costs more: Under F.S. 318.18(7), a toll citation adjudicated by the court carries a mandatory $100 fine plus court costs — the statutory reason a lost $2 toll fight costs far more than the toll ever did.

One insider note from my Florida Highway Patrol days: before you reflexively pay, understand how elections and adjudications interact with Florida's traffic school rules and the point system.

When It Gets Serious — Suspension and Registration Holds

Ignore the UTC and the machinery keeps grinding. Under F.S. 318.15, failing to respond, appear, or pay leads the clerk to notify the DHSMV, which suspends your driver license — the "D6" suspension — until you comply and pay a clearance fee. Separately, F.S. 316.1001(4) lets the tolling agency block issuance of your license plate or revalidation sticker, stopping your registration renewal dead. Unpaid balances can also go to collections.

The Stale-Address Trap

Every notice and citation travels by mail to the address on your FLHSMV registration, so people who moved without updating it routinely learn of a toll UTC only when a renewal is blocked or a traffic stop reveals a D6 suspension. If you've moved recently, check your driving record and registration status now.

The stakes of driving on that suspension just went up. As News4Jax and NBC Miami report, Isaiah's Law (HB 35), effective July 1, 2026, expands Florida's habitual traffic offender definition to include driving without a valid license — three qualifying convictions in five years brings a five-year revocation and third-degree felony exposure for driving afterward. A suspension that traces back to an ignored toll citation can now be the first domino toward felony-level consequences.

Common Issues Raised in Toll UTC Cases

Because the state's case rests on a toll enforcement officer's report and photographs, the presumption against the registered owner is rebuttable — and F.S. 316.1001 builds in specific ways to rebut it. These are general categories courts see, not legal advice for any particular case:

Issues That Come Up at the Citation Stage

  • Owner vs. driver: The owner may submit an affidavit establishing the vehicle was in another person's care, custody, or control, with protection for lessors of leased vehicles. A false affidavit is a second-degree misdemeanor — not a box to check casually.
  • Sold vehicles: Citations chasing a plate or vehicle transferred before the toll transaction.
  • Transponder and account errors: A funded SunPass or E-PASS account that should have covered the toll but didn't register.
  • Misread plates: Camera systems occasionally attribute a transaction to the wrong tag entirely.

Remember the Tropical Trailer lesson: these remedies attach at the citation stage, not the invoice stage. If you elect a hearing, here's what to expect at your first Florida traffic court hearing.

Which Stage Are You In? A Quick Self-Check

Look at the document in your hand:

  • An "invoice" or "toll enforcement notice" with a toll plus a small administrative fee: Stage One. Pay it, confirm the payment posted, and you're done — no points, no record, no court.
  • A Uniform Traffic Citation with a citation number and a 30-day deadline: Stage Two. Calendar the deadline and make your election under F.S. 318.14 before the window closes.
  • A blocked renewal or a D6 suspension on your record: The window already passed. You're facing F.S. 318.15 and F.S. 316.1001(4) consequences, and it's time to get help before you drive another mile suspended.
