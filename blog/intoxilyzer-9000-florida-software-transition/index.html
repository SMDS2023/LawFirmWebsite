New Year, New Training, New Breath-Test Challenges

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | June 25, 2026
DUI Defense Breath Testing

When Florida changes breath-test machines, the defense does not have to prove the new machine is bad. The first question is much simpler: did the State prove the new system was used correctly?

That is the point of the Intoxilyzer 9000 transition. A new year means new training. New training means new procedures. New procedures mean new places to test whether the breath result is actually reliable enough to use in court.

This is not about attacking science for the sake of attacking science. It is about foundation.

Before the government can rely on a breath number, it has to show that the right person used the right machine, under the right rule, with the right paperwork, after the right inspection.

When the machine changes, every part of that foundation deserves a fresh look.

The 9000 Is Not Just a New Box

FDLE's public materials show that the Intoxilyzer 9000 is being treated as its own system.

There is 9000-specific training. There are 9000-specific forms. There are 9000-specific operational procedures. The official 9000 affidavit includes a place for the instrument serial number and software.

That matters because DUI breath-test cases often turn on small foundation details. If the paperwork, training, inspection, or machine record does not line up, the breath result may be vulnerable.

The transition itself becomes part of the defense analysis.

New Training Means New Questions

One of the clearest facts from FDLE's 9000 curriculum is that Intoxilyzer 8000 and Intoxilyzer 9000 training are not interchangeable.

That is the high-level defense issue.

It is not enough to say an officer has used breath machines before. If the test was run on a 9000, the relevant question is whether the operator was properly trained and authorized on the 9000.

The same idea applies to inspection and maintenance. A machine can look official, sit in an agency breath-test room, and still require proof that it was properly inspected, registered, maintained, and operated under the current 9000 procedures.

New training creates new failure points.

New Forms Mean New Paper Trails

The 9000 transition also changes the paper trail.

FDLE/ATP Form 37a is the operational procedure for the Intoxilyzer 9000. FDLE/ATP Form 38a is the 9000 breath-test affidavit. Those forms matter because they show what the State is supposed to document when it asks a court to trust a breath result.

The defense does not need to overcomplicate the point.

If the government is relying on a 9000 result, it should be able to produce the 9000 paperwork.

That includes the operator's authority, the instrument records, the inspection history, the test sequence, and the information tying the result to the exact machine used.

The Software Field Is the Tell

I have not found a public FDLE download that appears to be the Intoxilyzer 9000 software itself.

But the official 9000 affidavit includes a software field.

That is the tell.

If the software version belongs on the official breath-test affidavit, it belongs in the defense review. The question is not whether software automatically creates a defense. The question is whether the State can connect the breath number to a properly approved and properly functioning instrument at the time of the test.

In a mature system, agencies often have a rhythm. They know the machine. They know the paperwork. They know the routine.

In a transition, routines change. That is where mistakes happen.

How the Result Can Be Attacked

A 9000 case should be reviewed for the same broad categories that matter in any breath-test case:

  • Was the operator properly trained and authorized?
  • Was the machine properly registered and inspected?
  • Were the required procedures followed?
  • Do the forms match the test?
  • Do the machine records match the affidavit?
  • Does the software/version information line up with the instrument record?
  • Was the agency still adjusting to the new system when the test was run?

Those are not technicalities. They are the foundation for the result.

If the foundation is weak, the number is weak.

The Takeaway

The Intoxilyzer 9000 transition creates a simple defense theme:

New year. New training. New procedures. New ways to challenge the result.

The State may ultimately be able to prove the breath test was valid. But it should have to prove it with 9000-specific records, not assumptions carried over from the Intoxilyzer 8000 era.

When Florida changes the machine, the defense should change the questions.

Charged With DUI in Florida?

If your DUI case involves a breath test, the number on the ticket is not the end of the story. The machine, the operator, the inspection records, the software field, and the paperwork all matter.
