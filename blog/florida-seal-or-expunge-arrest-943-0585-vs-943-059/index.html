Seal or Expunge a Florida Arrest Record?

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | November 6, 2026
Criminal Records Record Sealing Expunction

The choice between sealing or expunging a Florida arrest record usually turns on how your case ended, not on how serious the arrest sounded. Sealing keeps the record but makes it confidential, while expunction has agencies destroy it. Your prior record can rule out both, and the judge can still say no.

What Is the Difference Between Sealing and Expunction?

Sealing under F.S. 943.059 leaves the record in place. It becomes confidential and closed to public access. Criminal justice agencies, state judges deciding cases, and certain licensing and employment entities can still reach it.

Expunction goes further. Agencies destroy the record, but the Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE) must keep a confidential copy in every case. So neither remedy makes an arrest vanish from law enforcement entirely.

Our guide to clearing a criminal record in Florida covers the basics. This post focuses on the decision itself: which door, if any, is open to you.

Which Case Outcomes Point to Which Remedy?

Start with your final disposition, the court's record of how the case ended. Here is how the common endings usually sort under F.S. 943.0585 and the sealing law, assuming nothing else in your record blocks you.

  • No charges filed: An arrest with no charging document usually points to expunction.
  • Dismissed or nolle prossed: Nolle prosse means the prosecutor dropped the case. Either ending usually points to expunction.
  • Acquittal or not-guilty verdict: This also usually points to expunction.
  • Withhold of adjudication: This usually points to sealing, unless the offense is on the disqualifying list. Here is more on how Florida treats a withhold.
  • Adjudication of guilt: This closes both doors for that arrest.

Can a Sealed Record Later Be Expunged?

Sometimes. If a court ordered your record sealed, the expunction statute allows a later expunction in limited situations. A record sealed because adjudication was withheld, or because the charges were not all dismissed before trial, generally must stay sealed at least 10 years first.

The 10-year wait does not apply if no plea was entered, if all charges were dismissed before trial, or if a judge or jury acquitted you. The record must still otherwise qualify. Sealing has no matching exception, so an earlier order generally blocks a new sealing.

What Is Automatic Sealing?

Automatic sealing under F.S. 943.0595 is a separate track from court-ordered sealing. FDLE seals the record without a petition after the clerk sends a certified disposition. The clerk then keeps the related court record confidential.

It covers records with no charging document, with all counts dismissed or nolle prossed, or with a not-guilty verdict or acquittal on all counts. It excludes records from a charging document for a forcible felony or certain listed offenses. Certain statutory dismissals and not-guilty-by-insanity verdicts are also out.

It has limits. Other criminal justice agencies are not required to seal their records. The law sets no cap on how many times you can receive an automatic sealing.

Does Automatic Sealing Use Up Your One Chance?

The statutes do not say directly. The one-time limit in the court-ordered laws does not mention automatic sealing, and the automatic sealing law does not mention the limit. The 10-year path refers to court-ordered sealing. Do not assume either way. Raise the question before you apply.

Sealing vs. Expunction in Florida

What happens to the record
Sealing (F.S. 943.059)

The record stays but becomes confidential and exempt from public access.

Expunction (F.S. 943.0585)

Agencies destroy the record, while FDLE keeps a confidential copy.

Case outcomes that usually fit
Sealing (F.S. 943.059)

No adjudication of guilt on this arrest, including a withhold, unless F.S. 943.0584 bars it.

Expunction (F.S. 943.0585)

No charges filed, charges dismissed or nolle prossed, acquittal, or a not-guilty verdict.

Earlier seal or expunge order
Sealing (F.S. 943.059)

Generally bars another sealing.

Expunction (F.S. 943.0585)

Generally bars expunction, with a limited exception for a prior sealing under F.S. 943.0585(1)(h).

How to apply
Sealing (F.S. 943.059)

FDLE certificate of eligibility, then a court petition. The judge may still deny it.

Expunction (F.S. 943.0585)

FDLE certificate of eligibility, then a court petition. The judge may still deny it.

What Can Close Both Doors?

F.S. 943.0584 lists disqualifying offenses. They include murder, manslaughter, kidnapping, robbery, burglary of a dwelling, child abuse, human trafficking, drug trafficking, and aggravated battery. Those offenses bar both remedies when the record is a conviction.

For this list, a conviction includes a guilty or no contest plea, even if adjudication was withheld. So a withhold does not always save you. Check the current list, not a summary of it.

Other common bars come from your history or from the case itself:

  • A prior Florida adjudication of guilt: This generally blocks both remedies.
  • Certain juvenile adjudications: A delinquency adjudication for a felony or listed misdemeanor also counts.
  • An earlier seal or expunge order: The general rule is one time, subject to the 10-year path above.
  • Court supervision in this case: You cannot still be under court supervision tied to the arrest you want to clear.

How Do You Apply?

You start with FDLE, not the judge. You apply for a certificate of eligibility. The statutes set a $75 processing fee, which FDLE's executive director may waive, and a certificate is valid for 12 months. Confirm the current fee and procedures directly with FDLE.

With a certificate in hand, you file a petition in court. Florida Rule of Criminal Procedure 3.692 requires the certificate and a sworn statement. The court cannot order agencies to act until the certificate has issued.

A Certificate Is Not a Promise

Neither statute gives you a right to sealing or expunction. Even with a valid certificate, the court may deny your petition at its sole discretion.

What Won't Sealing or Expunging Do?

It will not hide the record from everyone. In many settings you may lawfully deny or not acknowledge the arrest, but the exceptions are real. They include criminal justice jobs, later criminal cases, Florida Bar applications, and listed licensing and employment settings.

A sealed record also must be acknowledged for firearm purchase checks and concealed-weapon licensing. If a job application is your worry, read whether you have to tell your employer about an arrest.

A new exception took effect July 1, 2026. Under ch. 2026-114, Laws of Florida, people screened through the Care Provider Background Screening Clearinghouse by a qualified entity can no longer lawfully deny these arrests. The same act lets independent sanctioning authorities, such as youth sports bodies, take part in the Clearinghouse.

What About Mugshot Sites?

A seal or expunge order deals with court and agency records. It does not by itself clear private websites, so your record may still show up online after the order.

F.S. 901.43 gives you a separate tool. You or a legal representative send a written request by registered mail to the publisher's registered agent. Include proof of identification and details identifying the photo. The publisher must remove it free within 10 calendar days.

That law reaches only publishers that take payment for removal, or whose primary business is publishing booking photos for commercial or pecuniary gain. Remedies include an injunction, daily penalties, and attorney fees. Our guide to mugshot removal in Florida has more detail.

Questions to Answer Before You Apply

  • What does the final disposition say about how the case ended?
  • Do you have any prior Florida adjudication of guilt, or a juvenile adjudication for a felony or listed misdemeanor?
  • Has any court ever sealed or expunged a record of yours, and when?
  • Has FDLE already automatically sealed this record?
  • Are you still under court supervision in this case?
  • Do you have certified copies of the disposition from the clerk, such as the Orange County Clerk of Courts?

These questions will not tell you the result. They show what needs checking first. This post is general information, not legal advice for your case.
