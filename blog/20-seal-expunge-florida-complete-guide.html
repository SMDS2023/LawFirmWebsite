1. Understanding the Difference: Seal vs. Expunge

This is the most common source of confusion. People use these terms interchangeably, but they're actually very different — and which one you pursue matters tremendously.

🔒 What is Sealing?

When your record is sealed, it still exists in the court system and law enforcement databases — but it's hidden from public view. Think of it like putting your file in a locked cabinet: it's there, but most people can't access it.

Who can still see a sealed record?

  • Law enforcement agencies (when you're arrested for a new crime)
  • Prosecutors and judges (in criminal cases)
  • Florida Bar (if you apply to be an attorney)
  • Department of Children and Families (certain positions)
  • School boards (certain education positions)
  • Other specific government agencies as defined by Florida Statute 943.059

When is sealing used?

Sealing is typically available for cases where you received a withhold of adjudication — meaning the judge withheld a formal conviction after you completed probation or other conditions. This is common in first-time offender situations where you took a plea deal.

🗑️ What is Expungement?

When your record is expunged, it's physically destroyed or removed from databases. This is the gold standard — it's like the arrest never happened.

Who can still see an expunged record?

  • Almost no one
  • Criminal justice agencies can see it if you're arrested for a new crime and they obtain a court order
  • FBI (for federal employment or security clearances, but they rarely look)

When is expungement available?

Expungement requires a higher standard: your charges must have been dismissed, dropped, or you were acquitted (found not guilty). You can also expunge a record that was sealed for 10+ years in limited circumstances.

⚠️ Critical Point: You Only Get ONE Shot

Florida law allows you to seal OR expunge ONE record in your lifetime (with very limited exceptions). That's it. If you have multiple arrests, you must choose strategically which one to clear. This is why consultation with an experienced attorney is so important.

2. Who Qualifies? Eligibility Requirements

Not everyone can seal or expunge their record. Florida has strict eligibility requirements. Let's break down who qualifies for each option.

✅ Quick Eligibility Self-Assessment

Answer these questions to get a preliminary idea if you qualify:

Requirements for SEALING

  • Withhold of Adjudication: The judge withheld formal conviction (this is noted on your disposition)
  • Completed Sentence: All probation, fines, community service, classes, and other conditions fully satisfied
  • No Prior Sealings/Expungements: This is your first and only use of this remedy
  • Not a Disqualifying Offense: Your charge is not on the statutory list of ineligible offenses (see below)
  • FDLE Certificate of Eligibility: You must obtain this from the Florida Department of Law Enforcement before petitioning the court
  • No Subsequent Convictions: You haven't been convicted of another crime since the case you want sealed

Requirements for EXPUNGEMENT

  • Charges Dismissed, Dropped, or Acquitted: No adjudication of guilt whatsoever
  • OR Previously Sealed for 10+ Years: In limited circumstances, a sealed record may be expunged after 10 years (rare)
  • No Prior Sealings/Expungements: This is your one-time opportunity
  • Not a Disqualifying Offense: Same list as sealing (see below)
  • FDLE Certificate of Eligibility: Required before petitioning court
  • No Subsequent Convictions: Clean record since the arrest you want expunged

🚫 Disqualifying Offenses (Cannot Be Sealed or Expunged Even if Dismissed)

Under Florida Statute 943.0584, certain offenses can NEVER be sealed or expunged, even if the charges were dropped or dismissed. These include:

  • Sexual battery (rape)
  • Lewd or lascivious offenses with minors
  • Child pornography
  • Child abuse
  • Aggravated child abuse
  • Sexual performance by a child
  • Computer pornography
  • Arson
  • Kidnapping/False imprisonment
  • Homicide
  • Manslaughter
  • Aggravated battery
  • Sexual misconduct by school employees
  • Trafficking in persons

Important: Domestic violence charges have complex eligibility rules. Some can be sealed/expunged if dismissed, but DV convictions generally cannot. DUI convictions can never be sealed or expunged, but DUI arrests that were dismissed may be eligible for expungement.

3. The Step-by-Step Process (How to Actually Do It)

Sealing or expunging your record is a multi-step process that typically takes 5-9 months from start to finish. Here's exactly what happens at each stage.

⏱️ Timeline Overview:

  • FDLE Certificate Processing: 3-4 months (longest wait)
  • Court Process: 2-5 months (after FDLE approval)
  • Total Time: 5-9 months typically
  • Factors Affecting Timeline: County backlog, State Attorney objections, whether a hearing is required

1 Obtain Certified Disposition

A certified disposition is the official court document showing the final outcome of your case. This is different from an unofficial "case summary" — it must have the clerk's official seal and certification.

How to get it:

  • In Orange County: Visit the Clerk of Court office (425 N. Orange Ave, Orlando) or request online at myorangeclerk.com
  • Other Counties: Contact the clerk in the county where your case was filed
  • Cost: Typically $2-5 per page (usually 1-3 pages total)
  • Time: 1-2 weeks for processing and mail delivery (faster if in-person)

Pro Tip: Request 2-3 certified copies. You'll need at least one for FDLE and one for your court petition. Having extras saves time if you make a mistake.

2 Apply for FDLE Certificate of Eligibility

The Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE) must conduct a statewide background check and issue a Certificate of Eligibility before you can petition the court. This is the longest step in the process.

What's required:

  • Application form: Download from FDLE.state.fl.us (different forms for sealing vs. expungement)
  • Fingerprints: Must be taken at an approved Live Scan location (list on FDLE website)
  • Certified disposition: The document from Step 1
  • Processing fee: $75 (non-refundable, even if denied)

How to submit:

  • Online through FDLE's portal (preferred method)
  • Mail to: FDLE, P.O. Box 1489, Tallahassee, FL 32302-1489

Wait time:

90-120 days typically. FDLE runs a complete background check to ensure you meet all statutory requirements. You can check status online with your tracking number.

Possible outcomes:

  • Approved: You receive Certificate of Eligibility and can proceed to Step 3
  • Denied: FDLE explains why (usually ineligible offense, prior sealing/expungement, or incomplete information)
  • More Information Needed: FDLE requests additional documentation (add 2-4 weeks)

Common Mistake: Applying to FDLE before completing all sentence requirements. If you still owe fines, haven't finished probation, or have pending community service, FDLE will deny your application and you'll lose the $75 fee. Make sure EVERYTHING is complete first.

3 Prepare Petition for Court

Once you have your FDLE Certificate of Eligibility, you must prepare a formal petition asking the court to seal or expunge your record.

Required documents:

  • Petition to Seal/Expunge: Formal legal pleading with specific statutory language
  • Proposed Order: The order you want the judge to sign
  • Certificate of Eligibility from FDLE: Original document
  • Certified Disposition: From Step 1
  • Civil Cover Sheet: Court administrative form

DIY vs. Attorney: You can prepare these documents yourself using forms available online, but mistakes here can result in denial or delay. Many people hire an attorney for this step to ensure accuracy.

4 File Petition with Court

File your completed petition in the same county where your case was originally filed.

Where to file:

  • Orange County: Clerk of Court, Criminal Division, Orange County Courthouse
  • Filing fee: Varies by county (Orange County is approximately $43-50)

Service requirements:

After filing, you must serve copies of your petition on:

  • State Attorney's Office (for the county)
  • FDLE (Tallahassee)
  • Arresting agency (FHP, OCSO, OPD, etc.)

Service is typically done via certified mail or through a process server. Keep your receipts — you'll need proof of service.

5 Await State Attorney Response

The State Attorney's Office has 30-45 days to review your petition and respond. They have three options:

  • Consent: They agree with your petition (best outcome — case proceeds smoothly)
  • No Response: They don't object within the deadline (treated as consent)
  • Objection: They oppose your petition and file a written objection (requires a hearing)

What I've Seen as a Former Prosecutor:

State Attorneys typically consent to petitions for minor offenses, first-time offenses, or cases that were dismissed. Objections usually happen when: (1) the offense was serious/violent, (2) there's a victim who objects, (3) you have subsequent arrests (even if not convicted), or (4) there's a technical legal issue with your petition.

6 Court Hearing (If Required)

If the State Attorney objects, or if the judge wants more information, a hearing will be scheduled. Not all cases require hearings — many are approved without one.

What happens at the hearing:

  • You (or your attorney) present your case to the judge
  • The State Attorney explains any objections
  • The judge asks questions
  • The judge makes a decision on the spot or takes it "under advisement" (decides later)

Judge's discretion:

Even if you meet all statutory requirements, the judge has discretion to deny your petition if they believe sealing/expunging is not in the "interest of justice." Factors judges consider:

  • Nature and severity of the original offense
  • Your conduct since the arrest
  • Whether you've completed rehabilitation (classes, therapy, etc.)
  • Impact on victims (if any)
  • Your employment/educational needs

Attorney Advantage: An experienced attorney knows the local judges, understands what arguments resonate in that courtroom, and can effectively counter State Attorney objections. This is where my background as a former law enforcement officer and current instructor helps tremendously — judges respect that perspective.

7 Order Entered & Records Sealed/Expunged

Once the judge approves your petition and signs the order, the clerk sends certified copies to all relevant agencies:

  • Florida Department of Law Enforcement (FDLE)
  • Arresting agency (FHP, OCSO, OPD, etc.)
  • State Attorney's Office
  • FBI (for expungements)
  • Any other agency that has records

Compliance deadline: These agencies have 60 days to seal or expunge the record as ordered by the court.

Verification: After 60-90 days, you should verify compliance by running a background check on yourself. Private background check companies may take 6-12 months to update their databases, as they rely on periodic public records refreshes.
