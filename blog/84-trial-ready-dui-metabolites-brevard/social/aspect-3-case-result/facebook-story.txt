From 14 Years to Zero: When Preparation Meets Opportunity

The call came on a Friday afternoon. My client's trial was set to begin Monday morning in Brevard County.

Maximum exposure: 14 years.

The charges were serious:
• 2 felony child neglect charges
• 2 DUI charges (one with property damage)
• 2 leaving the scene charges
• 3 traffic citations

The State's evidence seemed strong on paper. But when you dig into the details, problems emerge.

The breath test? 0.000 — zero alcohol.

The urine test? Positive for metabolites — the inactive substances that can remain in your system for days or weeks after use. Metabolites don't prove impairment. They prove prior use.

The Drug Recognition Expert? Certified for only about one year. Limited real-world experience evaluating drug impairment.

We'd been preparing for weeks. Multiple defense witnesses coordinated for trial. Cross-examination prepared. Science breakdown ready. We had a legitimate alternative explanation for what happened that night — one that didn't equal a DUI.

Then the prosecutor called: "We're requesting a continuance."

My response was immediate: "We oppose. Our witnesses are ready to appear."

Monday morning, the courtroom was packed. Thirty cases on the docket. When the State moved for a continuance and we opposed, the judge looked at the prosecutor and asked a simple question:

"Counsel, what's your position if the continuance is denied?"

That's when you know trial preparation has become leverage.

The case resolved that morning:

✓ DUI with property damage → Reduced to reckless driving with a withhold of adjudication
✓ Leaving the scene → Withhold of adjudication
✓ 2nd DUI charge → Nolle prosequi (dropped)
✓ 2nd leaving-the-scene charge → Nolle prosequi (dropped)
✓ All 3 traffic citations → Dismissed

Zero convictions. Zero jail time. Zero prison.

14 years of maximum exposure → 0 years of actual consequence.

That's what real defense work looks like. Not just filing motions and hoping. Not just showing up and asking for mercy. But actually preparing for trial — witnesses, cross-exam, science, strategy — so that when the moment comes, you have leverage.

Because when you're truly ready for trial, the State has to make a choice. And sometimes that choice is to resolve the case fairly rather than roll the dice in front of a jury.
