When Forensic Science Fails to Prove Impairment: The Metabolite Problem in Florida DUI Cases

In a recent Brevard County DUI case, the State's forensic evidence created more questions than answers.

The breath test: 0.000 — zero alcohol.
The urine test: Positive for THC metabolite, benzodiazepine metabolites, and amphetamine.

The State proceeded with a drug DUI prosecution.

Here's the legal and scientific problem:

Urine tests detect METABOLITES — the inactive byproducts left over after the body processes a substance. The THC metabolite found (11-Nor-9-carboxy-delta-9-THC) can remain detectable for 3-30 days after use. In frequent users, up to 90 days.

The presence of a metabolite cannot establish impairment at the time of driving. It can only establish prior use.

This distinction matters immensely in DUI defense. Blood tests show what's actively in your system. Urine tests show what was there at some indeterminate point in the past.

In this case, we were fully prepared for trial. Witnesses coordinated. Cross-examination prepared on DRE protocol. Science breakdown ready.

When the State requested a continuance the Friday before trial, we opposed it. At Monday's calendar call, the State had to make a choice: prove the case now or resolve it fairly.

The case resolved:
- DUI with property damage → Reckless driving (withhold of adjudication)
- 4 additional charges → Nolle prosequi
- All traffic citations → Dismissed

Zero convictions. Zero jail time.

The lesson for legal professionals: Forensic evidence is only as good as the science behind it. When prosecutors rely on metabolite-positive urine tests to prove impairment, the burden should shift back to the State to explain how inactive metabolites establish current impairment.

Often, they can't.
