The Friday Before Trial: When Preparation Becomes Leverage

It was the Friday before trial in Brevard County. I'd spent weeks preparing this DUI case. Multiple defense witnesses coordinated. Cross-examination prepared on the Drug Recognition Expert's training. Science breakdown ready on why urine metabolites don't prove impairment.

Then the prosecutor called: "We're requesting a continuance."

My response: "We oppose. Our witnesses are ready to appear for trial week."

Here's the thing about DUI defense — most of the work is invisible. The hours analyzing FDLE lab reports. The witness coordination. The research into metabolite detection windows. The trial preparation that forces the State to re-evaluate their position.

But when you're ready for trial — truly ready — the State has to make a choice.

Monday morning, there were 30 cases on the docket. When the State moved for a continuance and we opposed, the judge asked the prosecutor a simple question:

"Counsel, what's your position if the continuance is denied?"

This is the moment where invisible preparation becomes visible leverage.

The State's evidence:
• Breath test: 0.000 (zero alcohol)
• Urine test: Metabolites only (inactive substances that could have been from weeks prior)
• DRE certification: Only 1 year experience

How do you prove impairment with that?

The case resolved that morning:
✓ DUI with property damage → Reckless driving (withhold of adjudication)
✓ Leaving the scene → Withhold of adjudication
✓ 2nd DUI charge → Nolle prosequi (dropped)
✓ 2nd leaving-the-scene charge → Nolle prosequi (dropped)
✓ 3 traffic citations → All dismissed

Zero convictions. Zero jail time.

Sometimes, the best trial strategy is being so prepared for trial that you don't have to go to trial.

Because when the State knows you're ready — witnesses present, cross-exam prepared, theory solid — fairness becomes the easier path.
