Understanding Theft Charges in Florida

Theft charges in Florida carry consequences far beyond fines and potential jail time. A theft conviction—even for "petit" theft—creates a permanent record that can destroy professional careers, end graduate programs, and close doors to housing and employment for years.

What makes Florida's theft laws particularly dangerous is the hidden 5-year statute of limitations for retail theft under F.S. 812.035(10). While most misdemeanors have a 2-year limit, retail theft charges can surface years later—often at the worst possible moment in your career.

The Professional Career Trap

For graduate students, medical professionals, and anyone seeking professional licensure, theft charges require special attention. A withhold of adjudication—often presented as a "good" outcome—still requires disclosure on bar applications, medical board applications, and many professional licenses. The only truly acceptable outcome is dismissal.

Florida Theft Thresholds

Petit Theft (Misdemeanor)

  • Under $100: 2nd Degree Misdemeanor (60 days)
  • $100 - $749: 1st Degree Misdemeanor (1 year)

Still requires disclosure on most professional license applications.

Grand Theft (Felony)

  • $750 - $19,999: 3rd Degree Felony (5 years)
  • $20,000 - $99,999: 2nd Degree Felony (15 years)
  • $100,000+: 1st Degree Felony (30 years)
