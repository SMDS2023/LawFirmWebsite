Understanding Domestic Violence Charges in Florida

Domestic violence arrests in Florida trigger immediate and severe consequences that go far beyond most criminal charges. Under F.S. 741.2901, you face a mandatory "no bond" hold until First Appearance—meaning you cannot bond out until you see a judge, no matter how minor the alleged offense.

Even more critically, the judge at First Appearance will impose Pre-Trial Release (PTR) conditions that govern your life for months. Violating these conditions—even with the alleged victim's consent—can result in immediate incarceration for contempt of court.

Why Timing Matters

If you or a loved one has been arrested for domestic violence, the decisions made at First Appearance can affect your case for months. Having an attorney present—or at least prepared—before that hearing can make a significant difference in your PTR conditions.

What Happens After a DV Arrest

1

No-Bond Hold

You cannot bond out until First Appearance, typically the next morning. This is mandatory under Florida law.

2

First Appearance

A judge sets bond and imposes PTR conditions including no-contact orders and potentially residential exclusion.

3

PTR Compliance

Violating conditions—even with victim consent—can mean immediate incarceration. These conditions last until case resolution.
