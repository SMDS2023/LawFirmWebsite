Violation of Probation in Florida: F.S. 948.06

By Jeff Lotter, Criminal Defense Attorney | April 12, 2026
Probation Violations Criminal Defense Orange County

A violation of probation in Florida is a separate court case built on the claim that you broke a condition of your sentence. One missed check-in or a civil speeding ticket is not automatically a violation. But once your probation officer files a sworn affidavit, you face a new case with a lower burden of proof and usually no bond.

Is a traffic ticket or one missed check-in a violation?

Start with the two kinds of violation, which our earlier post on what a VOP really means covers in general terms. A substantive violation is a new crime committed while on probation. A technical violation is a broken condition of supervision: a missed report date, a curfew, an unpaid cost, a failed test, or a move you did not report.

The statute draws the line at criminal charges. A technical violation is any alleged violation that is not a new felony, misdemeanor, or criminal traffic offense. A civil ticket for speeding or a broken taillight is none of those, so by itself it is not a new law violation.

Criminal traffic is different. DUI, driving on a suspended license with knowledge, and reckless driving are crimes, and an arrest reaches your probation officer. Since July 2025, dangerous excessive speeding under F.S. 316.1922 also counts. Driving 50 mph or more over the limit is now a misdemeanor with a mandatory court date.

What does the State have to prove at a VOP hearing?

Less than at trial. The State proves a violation by the greater weight of the evidence, not beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge who sentenced you decides, without a jury.

The violation must also be willful and substantial. In State v. Carter (2002), the Florida Supreme Court held that a single missed monthly report can support revocation only if the State proves it was both. A missed appointment caused by a hospital stay, a car that would not start, or a documented lack of money is not automatically willful.

Money is its own category. In Del Valle v. State (2011), the court held that the State must prove you had the ability to pay before nonpayment can be willful. The judge must ask about your finances and make that finding on the record.

Hearsay comes in, but it cannot be the only proof. Russell v. State (2008) reversed a revocation where the only link to a new offense was an officer repeating what the victim said. In February 2026, the Second District Court of Appeal threw out a curfew violation proven only through relayed emails and texts.

What a genuine effort looks like

Florida courts have refused to call a violation willful when the person tried and failed. In Stevens v. State, the Third District reversed a revocation where the probationer made clumsy but genuine attempts to attend a required program.

How does a VOP case start?

Almost never in a courtroom. Your probation officer documents the missed report, positive test, unpaid cost, or new arrest and submits a violation report with a sworn affidavit to the sentencing judge. The judge signs an arrest warrant, and many people first learn of it at a traffic stop or their next report date.

Any officer who knows you are on probation can also arrest you without a warrant under F.S. 948.06.

Why is there no bond?

For most probationers, release on a VOP is up to the judge, not a right. The statute makes the hold mandatory for violent felony offenders of special concern and for people arrested for a qualifying offense while on supervision. In Orange County, VOP warrants are commonly endorsed no bond even when the statute does not require it.

Getting a bond set works much like the no-bond hold in domestic violence cases: your lawyer asks for a hearing and argues for release.

What happens at first appearance?

The judge reads the alleged violation and asks whether you admit or deny it. If you admit, the court can revoke, modify, or continue probation on the spot. If you deny, the court sets a full hearing where you have the right to a lawyer.

A violent felony offender of special concern also faces a separate written danger finding once a violation is proven, and a finding of danger requires revocation.

A new arrest is not a conviction

When the violation is a new charge, the State must prove the underlying conduct at the VOP hearing itself. An arrest report alone is not enough. Because the VOP needs less proof, prosecutors often try it first while the new case waits on discovery.

Since Russ v. State (1975), an acquittal on the new charge has not barred a revocation based on the same conduct. A dismissal later does not undo a revocation already entered. Once the affidavit and warrant issue, the probation clock stops, so you cannot wait out the original end date.

Technical vs. substantive probation violations in Florida

What it is
Technical violation

A broken condition of supervision that is not a new crime: missed report, curfew, unpaid costs, failed test, unreported move. A civil speeding or equipment ticket by itself is not a new law violation.

Substantive violation

A new felony, misdemeanor, or criminal traffic offense committed while on probation, such as DUI, driving while license suspended with knowledge, reckless driving, or dangerous excessive speeding under F.S. 316.1922.

What the State must prove
Technical violation

That the violation was willful and substantial, by the greater weight of the evidence. Inability to pay or a documented emergency is not willful.

Substantive violation

The underlying conduct itself, with non-hearsay evidence, at the VOP hearing. An arrest alone is not enough, and a later dismissal does not undo a revocation.

What does revocation cost?

The full original sentence. On revocation, the court adjudicates you guilty of the underlying offense and may impose any sentence it could have imposed at the start.

The scoresheet changes too. Under F.S. 921.0024, each community sanction violation adds 6 sentence points, and a violation that includes a new felony conviction adds 12. Those points can push a case that scored below the scoresheet's 44-point prison line over it.

The withhold problem

If your original sentence came with a withhold of adjudication, revocation converts it into a conviction. That conviction ends your eligibility to seal the case under Florida's sealing statute and blocks expunction with it.

Revocation is not the only outcome. The judge can modify or reinstate probation instead. For a nonviolent felony, the probation statute also lets the court send an eligible probationer to a mental health court or veterans treatment court program instead of prison. Eligibility depends on the offense and on amenability to treatment, including taking prescribed medication.
