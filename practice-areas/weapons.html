Florida is often described as a pro-gun state, yet Chapter 790 is packed with rules that can turn a simple mistake into a felony. This guide breaks the law into digestible pieces so you can exercise your rights without jeopardizing them.

Already charged? Violations carry severe penalties – and a skilled defense could mean the difference between freedom and prison. Use these sections to get oriented, then talk to counsel about the details of your case.

Key Definitions

Firearm

Any weapon – or readily convertible device – that fires a projectile by explosive.

Concealed Weapon

A handgun or other weapon carried so its presence isn’t visible to ordinary sight.

Securely Encased

Glove box (locked or unlocked), snapped holster, closed gun case, or similar container that must be opened to fire the weapon.

Readily Accessible

On your person – or so close it can be used as quickly as if on your person.

Who May Carry & How

1 · Concealed Carry (Permit & Permitless)

Eligibility at a glance:

  • 21 + (except active-duty / honorably discharged military)
  • No disqualifying felony or violent misdemeanor convictions
  • No potentially disqualifying open cases
  • Not on probation for a potentially disqualifying offense
  • Competency training (for licensure)

Permitless carriers must still meet the same eligibility and carry ID.

2 · Firearm in a Vehicle – s. 790.025

18 + may keep a handgun or other weapon in a private vehicle if it is securely encased... The law also allows you to carry the firearm inside a vehicle if the firearm is 'not readily accessible for immediate use'. I advise you to stick with securely encased. You may face prosecution by an anti-gun prosecutor or judge and your freedom is on the line.

  • Develop a habit to Always Carry Your Firearm Securely Encased in your vehicle!
  • Never securely encase your firearm in the same location as your vehicle's registration and insurance.

Police cannot require a concealed-carry license solely for a gun that is lawfully stored under this statute.

Exception: Firearms cannot be carried in a vehicle if otherwise prohibited (e.g., while subject to a domestic-violence injunction).

3 · Open Carry (Highly Restricted)

Generally illegal. Limited exceptions for hunting, fishing, camping, and brief inadvertent display by a lawful concealed carrier.

Common Exceptions for Lawful Carry

  • In your home or place of business
  • While fishing, hunting, camping, or traveling to/from these activities
  • Members en route to target practice or gun shows
  • Duly licensed manufacturers, dealers, & repairers
  • Law-enforcement officers on duty (and certain off-duty)
  • Military personnel on duty or training
  • Persons traveling with weapon securely encased from purchase to home or gunsmith
  • Firearm stored locked inside a vehicle in a workplace parking lot

Places You Cannot Carry (Even With a License)

  • 🏛️Courtrooms & polling sites
  • 🚔Law-enforcement stations & jails
  • 🎓Schools & college facilities
  • 🍻Bars (primary alcohol sales)
  • 🏟️Sporting events (non-firearm)
  • 🗳️Government body meetings
  • 🛫Airport sterile areas
  • 🚫Anywhere barred by federal law

Locked storage in your vehicle is generally allowed.

Secure Firearm Storage & Minors

Under s. 790.174, a loaded firearm must be kept in a locked box or secured with a trigger lock if a child under 16 is likely to access the home. Failure to do so – and the child gains access – is a 2nd-degree misdemeanor.

  • Safety devices are inexpensive compared to the legal & moral cost of an accident.
  • Proper storage may also be a favorable factor in plea negotiations or diversion eligibility.

Legal Defenses & Diversion Programs

Common Defenses
  • Illegal search / seizure – evidence suppressed if stop or search violated Fourth Amendment.
  • Lack of possession or knowledge – State must prove you knowingly possessed the firearm.
  • Statutory exceptions – e.g., securely encased vehicle carry or possession at home.
  • Self-defense justification – lawful defensive display or use.
  • Restoration of rights – for felon-in-possession cases.
Diversion & Alternative Resolutions

Pre-Trial Intervention (PTI) / Veteran or Mental-Health Courts can resolve certain non-violent gun charges without a conviction if the participant completes supervision, classes, and community service.

Upon successful completion, charges are dismissed and you may petition to seal or expunge the record – a critical step for preserving future firearm rights.

Open-Carry: What’s Happening in the Courts?

Florida’s open-carry ban survived a 2017 challenge (Norman v. State), but the legal landscape is shifting after the U.S. Supreme Court’s Bruen decision.

  • August 2024: Gun Owners of America and others filed a federal lawsuit claiming the ban violates the Second Amendment. (floridaphoenix.com)
  • January 2025: A bill to authorize open carry was filed for the 2025 Legislative Session. (thefernandezlawgroup.com)
  • State & federal prosecutors have moved to dismiss the lawsuit, citing the 2017 Florida Supreme Court ruling. (cbsnews.com)

Until a court or the Legislature says otherwise, open carry remains largely illegal in Florida.
