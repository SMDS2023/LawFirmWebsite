        DEFENDANT'S MOTION IN LIMINE TO EXCLUDE SCIENTIFIC INTERPRETATION
              OF HORIZONTAL GAZE NYSTAGMUS (HGN) TESTIMONY


        COMES NOW the Defendant, ANTHONY YAMEEN, by and through undersigned counsel,

and moves this Honorable Court for an order limiting and excluding certain testimony

regarding the Horizontal Gaze Nystagmus ("HGN") portion of the Standardized Field

Sobriety Tests. In support, Defendant states as follows:


                        I. INTRODUCTION AND RELIEF REQUESTED

        The defense respectfully requests that this Court exclude any testimony or

implication regarding the scientific interpretation, terminology, or statistical correlation of

HGN observations—such as the number of "clues," references to "accuracy," or correlation

to blood alcohol concentration ("BAC")—unless and until the State satisfies the evidentiary

foundations required by Florida law.

        The Defendant does not seek to exclude all mention of the officer's lay observations

(i.e., that the officer moved a pen and observed eye movements). Such lay testimony may

remain admissible for the limited purposes of establishing what the officer did and observed,

and for purposes of impeachment.


              II. LEGAL BASIS: HGN AS SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE UNDER
                                STATE v. MEADOR

A.      The HGN Test Constitutes Scientific Evidence

        In State v. Meador, 674 So. 2d 826 (Fla. 4th DCA 1996), the court held that

testimony about the correlation between HGN clues and alcohol consumption constitutes

scientific evidence. Unlike psychomotor tasks such as walk-and-turn or one-leg stand, HGN

relies upon medical and physiological principles not within a juror's common understanding.

        Therefore, before such evidence may be admitted, the State must establish:

        •       The reliability of the underlying scientific principle and test;

        •       The qualifications of the witness administering and interpreting the test; and

        •       The meaning of the results in the specific case.

(Id. at 831–32; see also State v. Strong, 593 So. 2d 1065 (Fla. 4th DCA 1992).)

        Because these predicates are not met here, the officer's interpretation of the HGN

test is inadmissible.


                  III. FAILURE TO SATISFY FOUNDATIONAL PREDICATES

A.      Lack of Reliable Administration

        The HGN's claimed reliability depends upon strict adherence to standardized

procedures. Officers are trained that any deviation from protocol invalidates the test's

scientific correlation.

        In the present case, the video and testimony will show the officer:

        •       Conducted the test while the Defendant faced flashing emergency lights, and

        •       Failed to document or acknowledge these distractions or other deviations.

        Such departures eliminate the reliability necessary for admissibility under Meador.


B.      Lack of Qualification to Offer Scientific Interpretation

        The officer in this case is not a Drug Recognition Expert (DRE) and lacks medical

or physiological expertise. SFST certification expressly states that the course does not

qualify an officer to interpret the causes of nystagmus or attribute it to alcohol.

        Because nystagmus may have multiple non-alcohol causes (e.g., fatigue, medications,

neurological conditions), the officer's attempt to testify about the "meaning" or "cause" of

eye movements exceeds the scope of permissible lay testimony.


C.      Risk of Prejudice and Misleading the Jury

        Admitting HGN testimony as though it possessed scientific reliability would imbue

the evidence with an aura of infallibility, misleading the jury to give it undue weight. Such

evidence should therefore be excluded under §§ 90.403 and 90.702, Fla. Stat., because its

prejudicial effect outweighs any probative value.


                     IV. REQUESTED LIMITATIONS ON TESTIMONY

        The defense respectfully requests that the Court limit the scope of permissible

testimony as follows:

1.      Exclude any reference to:

        •       "Clues," "points," "tests," "pass/fail," or numerical scoring;

        •       Scientific or technical terms (e.g., "lack of smooth pursuit," "onset prior to
                45 degrees");

        •       Any statement linking eye movement observations to specific BAC levels or
                statistical accuracy.

2.      Permit, solely for impeachment or context, testimony describing:

        •       That the officer attempted to administer a procedure involving movement of a
                stimulus before the eyes;

        •       The officer's visual observations (e.g., that the eyes appeared to move or
                jerk); and

        •       The officer's reliance on those lay observations in forming probable cause.


                V. PRESERVATION OF IMPEACHMENT VALUE (GOAL 2)

        The Defendant maintains the right to cross-examine and impeach the officer

regarding:

        •       Whether the HGN was properly administered;

        •       Whether the officer's report accurately documented what occurred; and

        •       Any inconsistencies between the report, field notes, body camera footage, or
                testimony.

        Florida law permits impeachment of a witness by prior inconsistent statements and

factual contradictions. See §§ 90.608–90.610, Fla. Stat. Evidence that the officer claimed

to perform standardized testing "by the book" when the video reveals otherwise is directly

relevant to credibility and truthfulness.


                                    VI. CONCLUSION

        Because the State cannot establish the scientific reliability, qualification, and

foundational meaning required under Meador, any testimony regarding the scientific

interpretation of the HGN test must be excluded.

        However, factual references to the administration and observation of the HGN may

remain admissible solely for impeachment and contextual purposes.

        WHEREFORE, the Defendant respectfully requests that this Court enter an order:

        1.      Excluding any scientific or technical testimony regarding HGN interpretation
                or statistical accuracy; and

        2.      Limiting the officer's testimony to lay descriptions of what was done and
                observed.
