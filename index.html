Frequently Asked Questions

After a DUI arrest in Florida, you typically only have 10 DAYS to request a formal review hearing with the DMV or waive your right to a hearing to potentially get a hardship license immediately. Failing to act within 10 days can result in an automatic license suspension. We can help you understand the best option for your situation.

While you can just pay a ticket, doing so is an admission of guilt and will likely result in points on your license, leading to increased insurance rates. An attorney can often negotiate to avoid points, or get the ticket dismissed based upon a legal technicality, or if you have a valid defense- the attorney can present that defense to the judge. Hiring an attorney ensures you get the best outcome, protecting your driving record and saving you overall in the long run.

Our founder, Jeff Lotter, brings over 20 years of combined experience as a defense attorney, former Florida State Trooper (including DUI unit), and former Deputy Sheriff. This unique background provides invaluable insight into law enforcement procedures. Additionally, we utilize a data-driven approach, analyzing thousands of local cases to identify trends and build stronger defense strategies for our clients.

We offer free initial consultations to discuss your case. For many common cases like DUIs and traffic tickets, we often provide flat-fee quotes so you know the cost upfront. Payment plans may also be available. The exact cost depends on the complexity of your specific case, which we can discuss during your consultation.

Leaving the Scene of an Accident (LSOA)

Hit and run charges occur when a driver leaves the scene of an accident without satisfying their legal obligations. A defense attorney can help by carefully reviewing the facts, evidence, and police reports to confirm whether the client was actually involved, can be properly identified, and if the legal oblications were met or if their departure from the scene was justified. The attorney will look for proof that the client knew of the crash. If the client left unintentionally or for a valid reason—like injury or fear—that can be a strong defense. They will also check if the client attempted to comply with the law by stopping or providing information. Additionally, the defense may challenge errors made by law enforcement during the investigation, which could lead to reduced charges or dismissal. With a skilled defense, it’s possible to beat your case or limit the strict consequences of a hit and run charge.

Super Speeder - Criminal Defense

As of July 2025, Florida's new Super Speeder law (FSS 316.1922) makes excessive speeding a criminal offense. If you're facing charges for driving 50+ mph over the limit or 100+ mph, you could be looking at jail time, a criminal record, and massive fines. We defend drivers charged under this new law by challenging speed measurements, attacking the legality of traffic stops, and negotiating charge reductions. Don't let one mistake ruin your future.
